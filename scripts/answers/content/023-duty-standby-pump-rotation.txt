## The direct answer
Pump rotation should be evaluated against the control sequence, equipment status, maintenance limits, and verified system response rather than changed solely to equalize runtime hours.

## Why this matters
An automatic changeover can reveal a latent fault or shift load in an unexpected way if operators do not know the sequence.

## Practical review points
- Identify lead-lag logic and rotation interval
- Review alarms and run-hour imbalance
- Verify standby readiness indicators
- Record observed response after an authorized change

## Example from operations
A scheduled rotation starts the standby pump but a flow alarm appears. The operator follows the alarm procedure and records the sequence instead of repeatedly cycling the pumps.

## Frequently asked question
### Does equal runtime prove two pumps are equally healthy?
No. Runtime is one indicator; condition, alarms, capacity, and verified performance also matter.

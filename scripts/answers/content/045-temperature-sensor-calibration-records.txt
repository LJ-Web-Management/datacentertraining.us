## The direct answer
A calibration record should identify the sensor, method, reference, date, result, tolerance, and corrective action so trend users know whether readings are trustworthy.

## Why this matters
Untraceable sensor data can undermine thermal decisions and make disagreements between dashboards difficult to resolve.

## Practical review points
- Assign each sensor a unique identifier
- Record method and reference instrument
- Document as-found and as-left results
- Flag out-of-tolerance readings for impact review

## Example from operations
A rack inlet sensor is replaced after a drift alert. The controls owner records the new device identity and checks whether historical trend comparisons remain valid.

## Frequently asked question
### Why does a calibration record need the sensor identifier?
It links the measurement evidence to the specific point operators and engineers rely on.

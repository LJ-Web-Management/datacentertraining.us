## The direct answer
A manual override should have an owner, reason, affected control point, expected duration, risk review, and a handback check visible to operations.

## Why this matters
An override that outlives its intended purpose can silently defeat automatic response and confuse later troubleshooting.

## Practical review points
- Record point, value, and reason
- Set an expiry and owner
- Show override status to every shift
- Verify return to intended control mode

## Example from operations
A technician holds a damper command during diagnostics. The shift handover lists the point, planned expiry, and criteria for returning it to automatic control.

## Frequently asked question
### Can an operator assume an override was removed when the work ticket closes?
No. Verify the actual point status and document the handback.

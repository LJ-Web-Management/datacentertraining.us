## The direct answer
Cross-training is most useful when it builds backup competence for defined tasks while preserving role limits, supervised practice, and formal authorization.

## Why this matters
Broad but shallow training can create overconfidence without reliable coverage for critical assignments.

## Practical review points
- Identify single-person knowledge dependencies
- Prioritize skills by consequence and frequency
- Pair learning with supervised practice
- Record limits and authorization status

## Example from operations
Only one technician understands a monitoring panel's escalation path. The manager creates a supervised backup plan and documents who may act during an absence.

## Frequently asked question
### Does cross-training mean everyone should be able to perform every task?
No. It should create defined backup capability within each person's verified limits.

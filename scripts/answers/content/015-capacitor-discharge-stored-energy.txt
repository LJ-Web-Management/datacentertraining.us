## The direct answer
Capacitors can retain hazardous energy after equipment is switched off; service planning must follow equipment-specific isolation, waiting, testing, and verification procedures.

## Why this matters
Assuming that off means de-energized can lead to shock or arc hazards during maintenance.

## Practical review points
- Identify stored-energy sources in the equipment documentation
- Use the approved energy-control procedure
- Require qualified verification before exposure
- Keep unauthorized personnel outside the work area

## Example from operations
A maintenance scope includes a power conversion assembly. The work planner identifies capacitor energy in the equipment documentation and confirms the required controls with the qualified lead.

## Frequently asked question
### Is switching off equipment enough to make capacitors safe?
No. Stored energy and verification requirements depend on the equipment and approved procedure.

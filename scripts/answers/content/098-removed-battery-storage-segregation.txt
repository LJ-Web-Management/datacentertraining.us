## The direct answer
Removed batteries should be identified, protected, and segregated according to chemistry, condition, site rules, and the approved disposition process.

## Why this matters
Damaged or mixed batteries can create handling, fire, chemical, and recycling risks.

## Practical review points
- Label chemistry and condition
- Keep damaged units in designated handling path
- Prevent contact with incompatible materials
- Track pickup and disposition records

## Example from operations
A replacement project stages used units in a corridor. The supervisor redirects them to the approved storage area and records the condition for the recycler.

## Frequently asked question
### Can damaged batteries be stored with routine removed units?
Use the site's specific damaged-battery procedure; do not assume routine storage is appropriate.

## The direct answer
Leak detection is actionable when each alarm maps to a clear physical zone, response owner, access route, and escalation path that operators can identify quickly.

## Why this matters
An alarm without an accurate location map delays response and makes it harder to protect nearby equipment.

## Practical review points
- Test that zone labels match physical areas
- Review map after layout or pipe changes
- Identify who responds and who can isolate flow
- Record sensor testing and map revisions

## Example from operations
A leak alarm points to a corridor after a rack project changed the cable path. The responder uses the updated map to narrow the inspection area.

## Frequently asked question
### What should a leak alarm map show?
A recognizable zone, nearby assets, response contact, and any approved isolation information needed for authorized responders.

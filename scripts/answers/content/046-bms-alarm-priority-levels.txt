## The direct answer
Alarm priority should reflect operational consequence, required response time, and the person responsible for action, not simply how alarming the message sounds.

## Why this matters
Poor priority design creates alarm fatigue and can bury conditions that require prompt attention.

## Practical review points
- Define severity criteria and response owner
- Review examples with operators
- Separate advisory and action-required messages
- Reassess priority after incidents or changes

## Example from operations
Operators receive frequent high-priority alarms for nonurgent status points. The controls lead reviews the priority matrix with the people who respond at night.

## Frequently asked question
### What makes an alarm high priority?
A documented consequence and response requirement, not a dramatic label or color alone.

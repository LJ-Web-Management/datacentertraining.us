## The direct answer
Cable trays and later installations should be checked for unintended obstruction of intended airflow paths, with any change reviewed against the facility's design and safety requirements.

## Why this matters
An airflow path altered by a later project can create localized hot spots that average room readings fail to show.

## Practical review points
- Review rack inlet and return-air paths
- Compare current layout with approved drawings
- Check impact on containment and sensors
- Coordinate corrective work with electrical and facilities owners

## Example from operations
A new overhead tray crosses a planned return path. The project team reviews thermal implications and cable access before approving the route.

## Frequently asked question
### Can a room-average temperature show whether airflow is blocked at one rack?
No. Local inlet measurements and airflow-path review may reveal conditions the average hides.

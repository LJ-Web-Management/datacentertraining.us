## The direct answer
A single-point-of-failure register should identify the component or path, the load it affects, how failure is detected, the consequence, and the approved mitigation or acceptance decision.

## Why this matters
A vague “redundant” label can conceal a shared dependency that maintenance or failure can take out.

## Practical review points
- Trace dependencies across power, cooling, controls, and communications
- Identify shared components and bypasses
- Assign an owner to each unresolved exposure
- Revalidate after design or configuration changes

## Example from operations
A project reports redundant cooling but both trains share one control panel. The reviewer adds the dependency to the register and requests an engineering disposition.

## Frequently asked question
### Does a redundant component automatically eliminate a single point of failure?
No. Shared controls, paths, utilities, or human processes may still be common dependencies.

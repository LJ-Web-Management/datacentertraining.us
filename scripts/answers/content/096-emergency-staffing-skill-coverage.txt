## The direct answer
Emergency staffing plans should identify minimum roles, required skills, alternates, callout triggers, access arrangements, and how one person can safely cover duties during a disruption.

## Why this matters
A roster can be full while critical decision or response skills are absent on a particular shift.

## Practical review points
- Map essential roles by scenario
- Identify qualified alternates and limits
- Review callout and access logistics
- Practice escalation when staffing is below plan

## Example from operations
An overnight illness leaves no controls specialist on site. The supervisor follows the staffing contingency and remote escalation path rather than assigning an unqualified substitute.

## Frequently asked question
### Can headcount alone show whether a shift is ready?
No. Verify role coverage, competency limits, and escalation support.

## The direct answer
A colocation responsibility matrix should state which party owns each asset, change, alarm, approval, communication, and emergency decision at the boundary between facility and tenant systems.

## Why this matters
Assumptions about ownership can delay response or lead one party to act on equipment controlled by the other.

## Practical review points
- Map shared and tenant-owned assets
- Define alarm routing and response times
- Set approval rules for changes
- Review boundary after service or contract changes

## Example from operations
A tenant sees an environmental alarm before the facility desk does. The matrix identifies who notifies whom and who has authority over the building system.

## Frequently asked question
### Should the facility operator act on tenant equipment alarms?
Follow the contract and site procedure; the correct action depends on ownership and delegated authority.

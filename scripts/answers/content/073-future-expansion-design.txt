## The direct answer
An expansion plan should reserve not just floor space but also power, cooling, cable routes, access, controls capacity, and work sequencing needed to add future loads safely.

## Why this matters
A physical expansion can be blocked by an overlooked upstream constraint or force rework near live equipment.

## Practical review points
- Map future demand against upstream capacity
- Reserve maintainable routes and work zones
- Document trigger points for investment
- Recheck assumptions when load forecasts change

## Example from operations
A planned rack row fits on the floor plan but exceeds the available distribution path. Capacity review catches the constraint before procurement.

## Frequently asked question
### Should floor-space availability be treated as deployable capacity?
No. Electrical, mechanical, access, and operational constraints also determine usable capacity.

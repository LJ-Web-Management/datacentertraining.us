## The direct answer
Construction access planning should separate work zones from live operations, define routes and escort requirements, and coordinate deliveries, tools, and emergency egress.

## Why this matters
Poorly managed construction traffic can expose live equipment, block response routes, or create security and safety gaps.

## Practical review points
- Map approved work and delivery routes
- Define barriers and escort points
- Check egress and emergency access
- Review route changes at daily coordination

## Example from operations
A material delivery route crosses an occupied white space. The coordinator changes the delivery window and route before authorizing the move.

## Frequently asked question
### Can construction teams use the nearest doorway for convenience?
Only if that route is approved and does not compromise safety, security, or ongoing operations.

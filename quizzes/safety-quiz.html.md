# Data Center Safety Quiz

Quiz · Safety

Ten questions on the hazards critical facility teams manage every day: energized equipment, batteries, fire suppression, refrigerants, and confined spaces. Each answer comes with a short explanation.

10 questions About 5 minutes Intermediate

## Quiz questions

1. Question 1 Under NFPA 70E, when has an electrically safe work condition been established?

   - As soon as the breaker is turned off and tagged
   - When the worker is wearing arc-rated PPE rated above the incident energy
   - After all sources are disconnected, locked and tagged out, and absence of voltage is verified with a properly tested meter
   - When an energized electrical work permit has been signed

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: after lockout and a verified absence of voltage.** Opening a breaker is only one step; stored energy must be released and the meter checked on a known live source before and after the test. Until absence of voltage is verified, treat the conductors as energized. [Data Center Electrical Safety Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-electrical-safety.html) walks through the steps.
2. Question 2 Under NFPA 70E, which reason can justify working on energized equipment instead of shutting it down?

   - The task will take less than 15 minutes
   - De-energizing would create a greater hazard or is infeasible because of equipment design or operational limitations
   - The worker is wearing arc-rated PPE
   - Scheduling an outage would be inconvenient for the customer

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: a greater hazard or true infeasibility.** De-energizing is the default, and justified energized work generally also needs an energized electrical work permit approved by management. Inconvenience or cost alone is not a justification, and PPE is the last line of defense, not a reason to work live. [Data Center Arc Flash Safety Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-arc-flash-safety.html) covers permits and justification.
3. Question 3 Who may perform justified energized electrical work in a data center?

   - Qualified persons, as determined by their employer through training and demonstrated skills
   - Any licensed electrician from any company
   - Anyone who has finished an online electrical safety course
   - Any employee wearing arc-rated PPE

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: employer-designated qualified persons.** OSHA and NFPA 70E describe a qualified person by demonstrated skills, knowledge, and safety training for the specific equipment and hazards. The employer makes that determination; a license or a course certificate alone does not. [Electrical Safety & Best Practices](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/electrical-safety-best-practices.html) explains how qualification works.
4. Question 4 An arc flash label on a switchboard lists an arc flash boundary of 48 inches. What does that mean?

   - The equipment door must stay closed whenever anyone is within 48 inches
   - The shock hazard ends 48 inches from exposed live parts
   - No one may come within 48 inches unless the equipment is de-energized
   - An arc flash could cause a second-degree burn inside 48 inches, so tasks there that could cause one need arc-rated PPE

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: inside it, an arc flash could cause a second-degree burn.** At the arc flash boundary, incident energy equals 1.2 cal/cm², roughly the onset of a second-degree burn on bare skin. The shock approach boundaries are separate distances on the same label. [Data Center Arc Flash Safety Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-arc-flash-safety.html) explains how to read labels.
5. Question 5 Under OSHA's lockout/tagout standard (29 CFR 1910.147), who normally removes a lockout device?

   - The supervisor at shift change
   - Any qualified electrician on site
   - The facility manager after the job is signed off
   - The authorized employee who applied it

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: the authorized employee who applied it.** The standard allows another person to remove it only under a specific, documented employer procedure, which includes confirming the employee is not at the facility and informing them before they return to work. Group lockout uses a lockbox so each worker keeps personal control. [Read the Data Center Safety Guide](https://datacentertraining.us/learn/data-center-safety.html).
6. Question 6 Why do flooded (vented) lead-acid battery rooms need ventilation?

   - To keep the electrolyte from freezing
   - Charging releases hydrogen gas, which can build up to flammable levels
   - To remove the carbon monoxide the batteries produce during discharge
   - Because lead-acid batteries go into thermal runaway every time they discharge

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: charging releases hydrogen.** Ventilation keeps hydrogen well below its lower flammable limit of about 4 percent in air. Lithium-ion batteries carry a different signature hazard, thermal runaway that can spread cell to cell and release flammable, toxic gases. [Data Center UPS and Battery System Safety Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-ups-battery-safety.html) covers both chemistries.
7. Question 7 What does pressing an emergency power off (EPO) button in a data hall typically do?

   - Transfers the room's load to generator power
   - Silences the fire alarm system for responders
   - Cuts power to the IT equipment it serves, often along with the room's HVAC
   - Shuts down the UPS batteries while utility power stays on

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: it cuts power to the IT equipment it serves.** An EPO is an emergency tool for responders, and it drops the load instantly, including UPS output to that area. That is why EPO buttons have covers and clear labels, and why accidental presses are a known cause of outages. [Data Center Emergency Power Off (EPO) Procedures Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-epo-procedures.html) covers safe use.
8. Question 8 The pre-discharge alarm of a clean agent fire suppression system sounds in the data hall. What should you do?

   - Leave right away, let the doors close behind you, and stay out until the room is cleared
   - Stay at your workstation, since clean agents are harmless at any concentration
   - Prop the doors open so the agent can vent out of the room
   - Shut down the servers one by one, then leave the room

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: evacuate and keep the doors closed.** The agent must hold its concentration in a sealed room to work, and a fire produces toxic smoke and decomposition products. Inert gas systems also lower the oxygen level, so re-enter only when the fire department or your site procedure clears it. [Data Center Fire Suppression System Safety Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-fire-suppression-safety.html) covers alarms and discharge.
9. Question 9 Under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146, what makes a confined space "permit-required"?

   - Any space with a locking door and no windows
   - Any space with less than 100 square feet of floor area
   - It is a confined space that also has, or could have, a serious hazard such as a hazardous atmosphere
   - Any space below ground level, whatever its hazards

   **Show answer**

   **Answer: a confined space plus a serious hazard.** A confined space is large enough to enter, has limited entry or exit, and is not designed for continuous occupancy. It becomes permit-required when it also contains, or could contain, a hazardous atmosphere, an engulfment hazard, a trapping configuration, or another serious hazard. [Data Center Raised Floor and Confined Space Awareness Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-raised-floor-confined-space.html) covers recognition.
10. Question 10 Why is a large refrigerant leak in a small, poorly ventilated mechanical room dangerous?

    - The expanding gas quickly raises the room temperature to unsafe levels
    - It is an environmental reporting issue, not a safety hazard
    - The cold vapor freezes the water pipes and sprinkler lines in the room
    - Common refrigerants are heavier than air and can displace oxygen, creating an asphyxiation hazard in low areas

    **Show answer**

    **Answer: it can displace oxygen.** Refrigerant vapor tends to pool in pits, basements, and low spots, and you may not smell it. Escaping liquid can also cause frostbite, which is why machinery rooms have refrigerant detectors and ventilation. [Data Center Cooling System and Refrigerant Safety Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-cooling-refrigerant-safety.html) covers the hazards.

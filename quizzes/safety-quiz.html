  1. Question 1Under NFPA 70E, when has an electrically safe work condition been established?
    Show answer

    Answer: after lockout and a verified absence of voltage. Opening a breaker is only one step; stored energy must be released and the meter checked on a known live source before and after the test. Until absence of voltage is verified, treat the conductors as energized. Data Center Electrical Safety Training walks through the steps.

  2. Question 2Under NFPA 70E, which reason can justify working on energized equipment instead of shutting it down?
    Show answer

    Answer: a greater hazard or true infeasibility. De-energizing is the default, and justified energized work generally also needs an energized electrical work permit approved by management. Inconvenience or cost alone is not a justification, and PPE is the last line of defense, not a reason to work live. Data Center Arc Flash Safety Training covers permits and justification.

  3. Question 3Who may perform justified energized electrical work in a data center?
    Show answer

    Answer: employer-designated qualified persons. OSHA and NFPA 70E describe a qualified person by demonstrated skills, knowledge, and safety training for the specific equipment and hazards. The employer makes that determination; a license or a course certificate alone does not. Electrical Safety & Best Practices explains how qualification works.

  4. Question 4An arc flash label on a switchboard lists an arc flash boundary of 48 inches. What does that mean?
    Show answer

    Answer: inside it, an arc flash could cause a second-degree burn. At the arc flash boundary, incident energy equals 1.2 cal/cm², roughly the onset of a second-degree burn on bare skin. The shock approach boundaries are separate distances on the same label. Data Center Arc Flash Safety Training explains how to read labels.

  5. Question 5Under OSHA's lockout/tagout standard (29 CFR 1910.147), who normally removes a lockout device?
    Show answer

    Answer: the authorized employee who applied it. The standard allows another person to remove it only under a specific, documented employer procedure, which includes confirming the employee is not at the facility and informing them before they return to work. Group lockout uses a lockbox so each worker keeps personal control. Read the Data Center Safety Guide.

  6. Question 6Why do flooded (vented) lead-acid battery rooms need ventilation?
    Show answer

    Answer: charging releases hydrogen. Ventilation keeps hydrogen well below its lower flammable limit of about 4 percent in air. Lithium-ion batteries carry a different signature hazard, thermal runaway that can spread cell to cell and release flammable, toxic gases. Data Center UPS and Battery System Safety Training covers both chemistries.

  7. Question 7What does pressing an emergency power off (EPO) button in a data hall typically do?
    Show answer

    Answer: it cuts power to the IT equipment it serves. An EPO is an emergency tool for responders, and it drops the load instantly, including UPS output to that area. That is why EPO buttons have covers and clear labels, and why accidental presses are a known cause of outages. Data Center Emergency Power Off (EPO) Procedures Training covers safe use.

  8. Question 8The pre-discharge alarm of a clean agent fire suppression system sounds in the data hall. What should you do?
    Show answer

    Answer: evacuate and keep the doors closed. The agent must hold its concentration in a sealed room to work, and a fire produces toxic smoke and decomposition products. Inert gas systems also lower the oxygen level, so re-enter only when the fire department or your site procedure clears it. Data Center Fire Suppression System Safety Training covers alarms and discharge.

  9. Question 9Under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146, what makes a confined space "permit-required"?
    Show answer

    Answer: a confined space plus a serious hazard. A confined space is large enough to enter, has limited entry or exit, and is not designed for continuous occupancy. It becomes permit-required when it also contains, or could contain, a hazardous atmosphere, an engulfment hazard, a trapping configuration, or another serious hazard. Data Center Raised Floor and Confined Space Awareness Training covers recognition.

  10. Question 10Why is a large refrigerant leak in a small, poorly ventilated mechanical room dangerous?
    Show answer

    Answer: it can displace oxygen. Refrigerant vapor tends to pool in pits, basements, and low spots, and you may not smell it. Escaping liquid can also cause frostbite, which is why machinery rooms have refrigerant detectors and ventilation. Data Center Cooling System and Refrigerant Safety Training covers the hazards.

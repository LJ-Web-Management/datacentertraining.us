  1. Question 1In data center terms, what is "white space"?
    Show answer

    Answer: the data hall where IT equipment lives. White space is the raised floor or slab area that holds racks, cabinets, and cages. The switchgear, UPS, battery, and mechanical rooms that support it are often called gray space. Read What Is a Data Center?

  2. Question 2What is an edge data center?
    Show answer

    Answer: a smaller facility close to users or devices. Edge sites put compute near where data is created or used, such as a metro area, a factory, or a cell site, so round trips are short. They are often remotely monitored and connect back to larger regional or hyperscale facilities. Data Center Design Fundamentals compares facility types.

  3. Question 3What is the main job of a UPS (uninterruptible power supply) in a data center?
    Show answer

    Answer: conditioning power and bridging to the generators. The UPS filters disturbances and, when the utility fails, carries the load from batteries or a flywheel for the seconds to minutes generators need to start. Transformers step voltage down, and a transfer switch moves the building to generator power. Read how UPS systems work.

  4. Question 4Which equipment cools the data hall by blowing room air across a chilled-water coil?
    Show answer

    Answer: a CRAH. A CRAH uses fans and a coil fed with chilled water from the central plant. Its cousin, the CRAC, has its own refrigerant compressor, while a PDU distributes power to the racks. Read the cooling guide.

  5. Question 5How is Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) calculated?
    Show answer

    Answer: total facility energy divided by IT equipment energy. A PUE of 1.0 would mean every kilowatt-hour reaches IT equipment, and a site at 1.5 spends 0.5 kWh on cooling, power losses, and lighting for every kWh of IT. The inverse, IT energy over total energy, is called DCiE. Read the PUE guide.

  6. Question 6Which Uptime Institute Tier is defined as "concurrently maintainable"?
    Show answer

    Answer: Tier III. Concurrently maintainable means any component or distribution path can be taken out for planned maintenance without shutting down IT. Tier IV adds fault tolerance, so a single unplanned failure does not affect the load either. Read the Tiers guide.

  7. Question 7In a hot aisle/cold aisle layout, how are the rack rows arranged?
    Show answer

    Answer: fronts face fronts, backs face backs. Servers pull cool air in the front and exhaust hot air out the back. Facing the rows this way keeps cold supply air in one aisle and hot exhaust in the next, so the two streams mix less. Data Center Design Fundamentals covers white space layout.

  8. Question 8What happens in a meet-me room?
    Show answer

    Answer: carriers and customers interconnect. The meet-me room (MMR) is the interconnection hub of a colocation or carrier facility. Network providers terminate their fiber there, and a cross-connect cable links a customer cage to a carrier or to another customer. Fiber Optics & Network Cabling covers the cabling side.

  9. Question 9A data hall needs four cooling units to carry its full heat load. The design installs five. What is that redundancy called?
    Show answer

    Answer: N+1. N is the capacity the load needs, here four units, and one extra unit makes N+1, so any single unit can fail or be serviced. A 2N design would install eight units as two complete sets. See the redundancy table.

  10. Question 10In a typical retail colocation data center, who owns and maintains the servers inside a customer's cage?
    Show answer

    Answer: the customer. A colocation provider sells space, power, cooling, physical security, and connectivity, while customers bring and manage their own IT equipment. Many providers also offer "remote hands" for simple tasks. Data Center Operations Management covers how the facility side runs.

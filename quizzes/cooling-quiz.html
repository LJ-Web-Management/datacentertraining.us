Data Center Cooling & HVAC Quiz

Ten questions on how heat leaves the data hall, from airflow management to chilled water math and liquid cooling. Each answer comes with a short explanation.

10 questions About 5 minutes Intermediate

Quiz questions

Question 1 of 10
Score 0
  1. Question 1What is the key difference between a CRAC and a CRAH?
    Show answer

    Answer: the CRAC has a compressor; the CRAH uses chilled water. A computer room air conditioner runs its own refrigeration cycle and rejects heat through a condenser. A computer room air handler is fans plus a chilled-water coil, so it depends on the chiller plant. Read the cooling guide.

  2. Question 2What is the ASHRAE recommended range for server inlet air temperature for most equipment classes?
    Show answer

    Answer: 18 to 27 °C (64.4 to 80.6 °F). The ASHRAE TC 9.9 thermal guidelines measure this at the server inlet, not at the cooling unit return. Allowable ranges are wider, and running cold aisles colder than needed wastes energy. Cooling Systems Design & Optimization covers the guidelines.

  3. Question 3A row of racks draws 35 kW. About how much cooling does that heat load need?
    Show answer

    Answer: about 10 tons. Nearly all power used by IT equipment becomes heat. One kW equals 3,412 BTU/hr and one ton equals 12,000 BTU/hr, so 35 kW is about 119,400 BTU/hr, or roughly 10 tons. Try the cooling load calculator.

  4. Question 4In a room with hot aisle containment, the open room outside the containment runs close to which temperature?
    Show answer

    Answer: the cold supply air temperature. Hot aisle containment captures exhaust behind the racks and returns it to the cooling units through a ceiling plenum or chimney, so the rest of the room becomes one large cold aisle. Cold aisle containment does the opposite: only the contained aisle is cool and the room runs warm. Cooling Systems Design & Optimization compares both approaches.

  5. Question 5What is bypass airflow?
    Show answer

    Answer: supply air that never passes through IT equipment. Bypass air is cooling capacity you paid for that never touches a server. Common causes are unsealed cable cutouts, perforated tiles in hot aisles, and supplying more air than the servers draw, while hot air curling into inlets is recirculation, the opposite problem. Energy Efficiency & PUE Optimization covers airflow fixes.

  6. Question 6Why are blanking panels installed in empty rack spaces?
    Show answer

    Answer: to stop exhaust from recirculating to server intakes. Open rack units let hot air flow from the back of the rack to the front, where servers pull it in, and let cold air short-circuit to the hot side. Blanking panels are one of the cheapest airflow fixes in a data hall. HVAC Systems Troubleshooting Essentials covers hot spot diagnosis.

  7. Question 7What does an economizer do in a data center cooling system?
    Show answer

    Answer: it uses outdoor conditions to cut compressor cooling. An airside economizer uses outdoor air directly or through a heat exchanger. A waterside economizer uses cooling towers or dry coolers to cool the chilled water loop through a heat exchanger, so the chillers can slow down or shut off. Energy Efficiency & PUE Optimization covers free cooling.

  8. Question 8For water, heat flow is roughly BTU/hr = 500 × gpm × delta T (°F). If the chilled water delta T falls by half while the load stays the same, what happens to the flow needed?
    Show answer

    Answer: it doubles. With a fixed load, flow and delta T are inversely related, so half the delta T needs twice the water. That is why "low delta T syndrome" drives up pump energy and can force extra chillers online; dirty coils, open bypass valves, and poor setpoints are common causes. Mechanical Systems & Equipment Maintenance covers the chilled water plant.

  9. Question 9In a direct-to-chip liquid cooling system, what does the coolant distribution unit (CDU) do?
    Show answer

    Answer: it isolates and controls the coolant loop to the cold plates. The CDU uses a heat exchanger and pumps to keep the clean, tightly controlled technology cooling loop separate from the facility water system. It also filters the loop and alarms on leaks or pressure changes. Read how AI data centers use liquid cooling.

  10. Question 10Under EPA Section 608 of the Clean Air Act, who needs Section 608 technician certification?
    Show answer

    Answer: technicians whose work could release refrigerant. Section 608 certification is earned through EPA-approved certifying programs in Type I, II, III, or Universal categories, based on the equipment serviced. Data Center Cooling System and Refrigerant Safety Training covers refrigerant hazards and handling awareness; it does not replace Section 608 certification.

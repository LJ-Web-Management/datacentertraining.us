Your best-fit data center career

Your match

Critical Power Technician or Data Center Electrician

You are drawn to the electrical backbone of the facility: switchgear, UPS systems, batteries, and generators. Critical power technicians keep that chain healthy, perform switching under strict procedures, and are the first responders when power misbehaves.

  • Day to day: inspections, UPS and battery checks, generator tests, and planned switching.
  • Typical path in: an electrical apprenticeship or license, or military electrical experience.
  • Where it leads: lead technician, electrical supervisor, or chief engineer.

Your match

Critical Facilities HVAC or Mechanical Technician

You like equipment you can put your hands on and problems you can measure. Every watt in a data center becomes heat, and mechanical technicians keep chillers, CRAHs, pumps, and airflow working so the IT load never overheats.

  • Day to day: rounds on cooling equipment, preventive maintenance, troubleshooting alarms, and airflow fixes.
  • Typical path in: HVAC or refrigeration training, plus EPA Section 608 certification for refrigerant work.
  • Where it leads: senior mechanical technician, chief engineer, or efficiency specialist.

Where to start:

Your match

Critical Facilities Operations, From Technician to Manager

You are the steady hand people call when something goes wrong. Operations staff run the site around the clock: they follow and write procedures, coordinate maintenance and vendors, lead incident response, and keep uptime promises.

  • Day to day: shift rounds, work orders, change management, and incident response.
  • Typical path in: facilities maintenance, building engineering, or military operations experience.
  • Where it leads: shift lead, facility manager, and director of critical facilities.

Where to start:

Your match

Data Center Security and Compliance

You notice details others miss and you are comfortable enforcing rules. Data centers protect some of the world's most valuable equipment and data, and security and compliance teams control who gets in, document everything, and keep the site audit-ready.

  • Day to day: access control, visitor escort, monitoring cameras and alarms, and audit evidence.
  • Typical path in: security, law enforcement, military, or compliance experience.
  • Where it leads: security supervisor, compliance specialist, or security manager.

Where to start:

Your match

Data Center Design and Commissioning Engineer

You like to think a system through before it exists. Design engineers, planners, and commissioning agents decide how much redundancy a facility needs, how power and cooling are laid out, and prove it all works before the first server arrives.

  • Day to day: design reviews, capacity models, test scripts, and witnessing commissioning tests.
  • Typical path in: an engineering degree, construction project management, or senior field experience.
  • Where it leads: commissioning lead, design manager, or capacity planning lead.

Where to start:

Your match

Monitoring, DCIM, and Data Center Technician

You think in systems and data. Monitoring and data center technicians watch the BMS and DCIM platforms, chase down alarms, manage cabling and capacity records, and connect what the building is doing to what the IT load needs.

  • Day to day: alarm response, DCIM records, cabling and rack work, and trend analysis.
  • Typical path in: IT help desk, networking, or building controls experience.
  • Where it leads: NOC lead, controls or DCIM specialist, or capacity analyst.

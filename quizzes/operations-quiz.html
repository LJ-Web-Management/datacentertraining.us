  1. Question 1Which document is a step-by-step plan for a specific, planned change to live critical infrastructure, such as putting a UPS on maintenance bypass?
    Show answer

    Answer: a MOP. SOPs cover routine, repeatable tasks like equipment rounds, while MOPs cover planned work on critical systems with exact steps, hold points, approvals, and a back-out plan. EOPs tell responders what to do when something fails. Read the operations guide.

  2. Question 2Which statement about change management in a critical facility is correct?
    Show answer

    Answer: every change is assessed, approved, and documented first. Many outages start with a change or maintenance task, so change review is where risk gets caught. Emergency changes still follow an expedited path and are documented afterward. Data Center Operations Management covers change control.

  3. Question 3What is the difference between preventive and predictive maintenance?
    Show answer

    Answer: schedule-based versus condition-based. Preventive work follows the calendar or run hours, like quarterly filter changes. Predictive work watches condition, like rising bearing vibration or a hot lug on an infrared scan, and fixes it before it fails; repairing after failure is reactive maintenance. Preventive Maintenance Planning covers both strategies.

  4. Question 4What does a CMMS do for a facilities team?
    Show answer

    Answer: it manages maintenance work and asset history. A computerized maintenance management system is the system of record for what was done, when, and by whom. Real-time control belongs to the BMS, and rack-level capacity tracking belongs to DCIM. Preventive Maintenance Planning covers CMMS use.

  5. Question 5Inherent availability is often estimated as MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR). What does shortening MTTR do?
    Show answer

    Answer: it raises availability. MTBF is mean time between failures and MTTR is mean time to repair. With an MTBF of 10,000 hours, cutting MTTR from 10 hours to 2 hours lifts availability from about 99.90 to 99.98 percent, which is why on-site spares, clear EOPs, and trained staff matter. Incident Response & Troubleshooting covers faster recovery.

  6. Question 6What is the difference between RTO and RPO?
    Show answer

    Answer: RTO is restore time; RPO is data loss. An RPO of 15 minutes means backups or replication can never be more than 15 minutes behind. RTO drives recovery site and staffing decisions, while RPO drives backup and replication design. Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity covers both.

  7. Question 7Which system is built to monitor the electrical distribution system in detail, including power quality and breaker status, often with waveform capture?
    Show answer

    Answer: the EPMS. An EPMS reads meters, relays, and breaker trip units to capture power quality events and sequence-of-events data. A BMS monitors and controls mechanical systems like CRAHs, chillers, and pumps, and DCIM ties facility data to IT assets and capacity. Monitoring, Automation & BMS Systems compares the platforms.

  8. Question 8What is the best way to reduce alarm fatigue in a monitoring center?
    Show answer

    Answer: rationalize the alarms. When operators see hundreds of low-value alarms, they start acknowledging without reading, and a critical one gets missed. Rationalization gives every alarm a cause, a priority, and a defined response. DCIM Platform Fundamentals covers alarm management.

  9. Question 9After an incident is resolved, what is the goal of a root cause analysis (RCA)?
    Show answer

    Answer: find underlying causes and prevent a repeat. Methods like the 5 Whys and fishbone diagrams dig past "the breaker tripped" to why it tripped and why nobody caught it. A blameless approach gets honest answers, and corrective actions are tracked to closure. Incident Response & Troubleshooting covers RCA methods.

  10. Question 10What makes a good shift handoff in a 24/7 critical facility?
    Show answer

    Answer: a structured, written, face-to-face turnover. Incidents can start when the incoming shift does not know a system was left in bypass or a valve was left closed. A written log plus a walk-through keeps that knowledge from leaving with the outgoing shift. Data Center Operations Management covers shift practices.

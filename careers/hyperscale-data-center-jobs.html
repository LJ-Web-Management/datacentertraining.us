Data Center Jobs at Amazon, Google, Microsoft & Meta

The biggest cloud and AI companies hire thousands of technicians, tradespeople, and engineers to run their data centers. Here are the job titles they actually use, the training programs they publicly run, where to search, and what their interviews tend to test.

Short answer: Data center jobs at Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and Meta fall into the same few families: IT hardware technicians, critical facilities technicians and engineers (electrical, mechanical, controls), security, construction and commissioning, and operations management. Each company posts openings on its own careers site under its own titles, such as AWS's Data Center Engineering Operations Technician or Microsoft's Critical Environment Technician.

Key takeaways

  • Search each company's careers site with its own titles. "Critical Environment" means facilities at Microsoft; "Engineering Operations" means facilities at AWS.
  • Most technician postings ask for hands-on troubleshooting experience, a willingness to work nights, weekends, and holidays, and the ability to lift and move heavy equipment. Many do not require a degree.
  • Several companies run public pathways: AWS's paid pre-apprenticeship, Microsoft's Datacenter Academy and pathways, Meta's America's Workforce Academy, and Oracle's military programs.
  • A lot of the work, especially construction, security, and some technician roles, is done by contractors and staffing firms, so the employer on your paycheck may not be the company on the building.

Amazon and AWS data center jobs

Amazon's data center jobs sit inside AWS Infrastructure Services, the group that designs, builds, and operates AWS data centers. The two titles most job seekers want are Data Center Technician and Data Center Engineering Operations Technician.

Data Center Technician (IT side)

AWS describes data center technicians as doing hardware and network repair, installing equipment, creating documentation, and fixing problems in a space that runs 24/7, with travel inside and outside the regional work area. A U.S. posting we reviewed listed basic qualifications of a high school diploma or equivalent, one or more years of computer or server hardware troubleshooting or related IT experience, and a flexible schedule including weekends, nights, and holidays. Preferred qualifications included Layer 1 and Layer 2 network troubleshooting, an associate's degree or equivalent experience, ticketing systems, and experience in a data center or other critical environment.

Data Center Engineering Operations Technician (facilities side)

DCEO technicians keep the power, cooling, and fire and life safety systems running. An AWS posting lists rack installs and decommissioning, operational readings, troubleshooting facility and rack-level events within internal service level agreements, monitoring maintenance on electrical, mechanical, and fire and life safety equipment, managing contractors and vendors on site, and supporting ticketing, root cause analysis, change management, procedure writing, and building management systems. Shifts can run up to 12 hours on a rotating schedule. The basic qualification is a year or more of electrical or mechanical facilities maintenance.

AWS also posts cleared versions of both roles for sites that require a U.S. government security clearance, plus Data Center Security Specialist, Data Center Chief Engineer, Data Center Facility Manager, commissioning engineer, and construction roles.

AWS training programs

AWS has funded technician training at community colleges near its data centers. One verified example: Germanna Community College in Virginia hosts the AWS Information Infrastructure Pre-Apprenticeship (i2PA), a four-week program with hands-on instruction in electrical, HVAC, mechanical, and data center operations. Germanna says its students are selected and paid by Amazon. Similar programs run at other colleges, so check the college nearest the AWS region you want to work in.

How Amazon hires

Amazon's How We Hire page says the process differs by role and can include an online application, assessments, a phone screen, and an interview loop. Amazon also publishes its Leadership Principles, which its interviewers use, so behavioral questions are a given. If you are asked to take an online assessment for a technician role, the recruiter will tell you what to expect; Amazon does not publish a technician-specific assessment guide that we could find.

Google data center jobs

Google splits its data center workforce into server operations (the IT hardware) and data center facilities (the building systems). Both sit within its technical infrastructure organization.

Data Center Technician

Google's technician postings describe installing, configuring, testing, troubleshooting, and maintaining servers and their components, plus server software such as Google's Linux cluster, and configuring networking gear. Responsibilities include deploying new infrastructure, following data center procedures to diagnose equipment issues, disassembling end-of-life equipment, preventive maintenance, and tracking storage media so data is properly erased under Google's security standards.

Minimum qualifications in U.S. postings include experience with component-level repairs on technical equipment, experience troubleshooting operating systems, server hardware, or networking protocols, the ability to lift and move 50 lb (23 kg) of equipment, and the ability to work non-standard hours including nights, weekends, and holidays. A bachelor's degree is listed as preferred, alongside "equivalent practical experience," and Linux experience is a common preferred skill. Google posts Technician I, II, and III levels, including roles on its AI Infrastructure team.

Data Center Facilities Technician

Google posts facilities technician roles by specialty: Electrical, Mechanical, Controls, Fire Life Safety, and Generators. An electrical posting describes operating, monitoring, and supporting facility conditions, including cooling, power supply, generators, UPS systems, and electrical distribution, and managing performance issues and outages. Its minimum qualifications include an associate's degree, trade school certification, or equivalent practical experience; several years of electrical experience in an industrial or commercial environment; and experience with protective relays or high voltage. Preferred knowledge includes feeders, transformers, switchgear, UPS systems, ATS and STS units, and PDUs, which is exactly the chain explained in our data center power guide.

Internships and hiring

Google has posted 2027 internship listings for both Data Center Technician and Data Center Facilities Technician roles; read each listing for eligibility. Google's How we hire page explains its process and offers interview prep. Some U.S. postings state the role is not eligible for immigration sponsorship and must be worked fully on site.

Microsoft data center jobs

Microsoft spells it "datacenter" and uses two main technician titles: Data Center Technician for IT hardware and Critical Environment Technician (CET) for facilities.

  • Data Center Technician: Microsoft says this role manages and maintains the hardware infrastructure: setting up and configuring servers, troubleshooting technical issues, conducting inspections, and handling hardware upgrades. It lists completion of high school, an apprenticeship or vocational qualification, or an equivalent, plus basic computer hardware knowledge.
  • Critical Environment Technician: Microsoft calls the CET one of the most common roles on its datacenter operations team. CETs oversee critical infrastructure such as cooling, power, and security, with duties that include equipment setup, emergency response, and documentation. Microsoft lists electricians, electrical and mechanical technicians, maintenance technicians, marine engineers, facility engineers, shift engineers, and HVAC technicians as strong backgrounds.
  • Other titles: Senior Critical Environment Technician, Critical Environment Electrical Engineer, Critical Environment Mechanical Engineer, Critical Environment Operations Manager, Data Center Technician Manager, and Data Center Security Operations Manager. Some postings are for cleared government sites.

Microsoft Datacenter Academy and pathways

Microsoft's Datacenter Pathways page lists several routes into technician jobs:

  • Microsoft Datacenter Academy: programs with partner colleges in the U.S. that Microsoft says offer training, certifications, scholarships, grants, and mentorship. At Estrella Mountain Community College in Arizona, for example, the academy names CompTIA A+, Server+, Network+, and Security+ as baseline certifications and says students can interview for work experience programs inside a Microsoft datacenter.
  • Military Datacenter Pathway: for transitioning service members in their final 180 days who are eligible for the DoD SkillBridge program.
  • Neurodiversity Datacenter Pathway and Communities Datacenter Pathway, the latter aimed at seniors and alumni of Historically Black Colleges and Universities.

Meta data center jobs

Meta's data center site says it hires "electricians, HVAC specialists, server and network techs, safety and security experts, engineers, program managers, analysts and more" to design, build, and run its data centers. Openings are listed on Meta Careers; search "data center" plus the city of the campus you want.

Meta's biggest public workforce program is America's Workforce Academy, announced in June 2026. Meta describes it as a cost-free skilled trades program with an initial $115 million first-year investment, a job guarantee for graduates who meet its requirements, and two credentials: a National Center for Construction Education and Research (NCCER) credential and an America's Workforce Certificate. The 2026 pilot locations are Louisiana, Ohio, Indiana, and Texas, with partners including the National Urban League, Associated Builders and Contractors, and CBRE. Trade press coverage notes the jobs are on Meta data center projects through Meta's contractor partners, not direct Meta employment. Meta also cites an earlier fiber installation training program called Level-Up, and it sponsors Be Pro Be Proud mobile skilled trades workshops in several of its data center states.

If you are searching for Meta's data center training program, start with America's Workforce Academy and our data center apprenticeships guide.

Oracle data center jobs

Oracle hires for Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) data centers through its main careers site. Current U.S. titles include Data Center Technician, Senior and Lead Data Center Support Services Technician, Data Center Operator, Data Center Facilities Operations Technician (Electrical, Mechanical, and General), Data Center Facilities Engineer, Data Center Commissioning Engineer, Data Center Low Voltage Engineer, and Site Lead, Data Center Operations. Oracle summarizes its support services technician role as hands-on work supporting hardware, network connectivity, critical infrastructure, troubleshooting, and day-to-day data center operations.

Oracle also advertises military pathways. A technician posting describes a partnership with Washington Vets to Tech (WaV2T) for service members, veterans, and military spouses who complete a data center technician pathway, and another posting describes a Catalyst Military Program with scheduled cohort start dates. Some Oracle facilities postings require U.S. citizenship, so read the requirements closely.

Colocation and AI cloud employers

Hyperscalers are not the only large employers. Colocation companies run data centers that many tenants share, and AI cloud providers run GPU-dense sites. Their titles lean toward "critical facilities," and many are open to people with trade or military backgrounds.

  • Equinix: posts roles such as Data Center Critical Facilities Engineer, Critical Facilities Technician, Customer Operations Technician, and Logistics Technician on Equinix Careers, which also mentions opportunities for apprentices and students.
  • Digital Realty: in May 2026 announced New Hire Accelerate Pathways, a standardized onboarding program delivered with DCD Academy that requires no prior technical experience. Digital Realty says each participant earns the Data Center Technician (DCT) certification issued by DCD Academy. Openings are on Digital Realty Careers.
  • Vantage Data Centers: posts Critical Facilities Technician, Electrical Technician, HVAC Technician, Automation & Controls Technician, and Facility Technician roles, and says it recruits for co-op, apprenticeship, and internship programs. See Vantage Careers.
  • CoreWeave: an AI cloud provider that posts Data Center Technician roles at many U.S. sites, plus command center, security, and engineering roles, on CoreWeave Careers.
  • AI labs: companies such as Anthropic post data center electrical, mechanical, and controls engineering, capacity, and operations roles on their careers pages. These are mostly engineering and program roles rather than on-site technician jobs.

Facilities services firms also run many sites for owners. For a deeper look at the AI side, read AI data center jobs.

How do you find and apply for hyperscale data center jobs?

Go straight to each company's official careers site, search with its own titles, and set alerts for the campuses near you. Here is a process that works:

  1. Search by the company's own title

    Use "Data Center Engineering Operations" on amazon.jobs, "Data Center Facilities Technician" on Google Careers, and "Critical Environment" on Microsoft Careers. A generic "data center technician" search misses most facilities roles.

  2. Filter by location and set alerts

    Large campuses are often in small towns. Our data center jobs by location guide shows where operators have announced or run facilities.

  3. Read minimum versus preferred qualifications

    Minimums are the bar to apply. Preferred items, like a degree or Linux experience, are where you can stand out. Match your resume's wording to the posting; our technician resume guide shows how.

  4. Check contractors and staffing firms too

    Construction, commissioning, security, and some technician work is done by contractors and staffing firms on behalf of the owner. Meta's America's Workforce Academy, for example, places graduates with contractor partners. Those postings sometimes describe the site only as "a hyperscale data center."

  5. Read the pay range in the posting

    Many U.S. postings from these companies include a pay range, and several states require one. That range is a better guide than any third-party estimate.

  6. Apply only through official channels

    Big tech companies do not charge application or training fees. If a "recruiter" asks for money or banking details before an offer, walk away and apply through the company's own site.

What do postings ask for physically and on schedule?

Expect real physical requirements. Google technician postings ask candidates to lift and move 50 lb of equipment and to bend, kneel, and climb for extended periods. AWS postings mention ladders, uneven job sites, noisy spaces, group lifts for heavier items, and shifts longer than 8 hours. Nearly every posting asks for nights, weekends, holidays, or rotating shifts, because data centers never close.

Interview preparation

Interviews for these roles usually test four things: how you troubleshoot, what you know about the hardware or building systems, how seriously you take safety and procedure, and how you behave on a team. Companies do not publish technician question lists, so prepare for the skills, not a script.

  • Troubleshooting: expect to talk through a failure out loud. For IT roles, that might be a server that will not boot or a link that is down; work from the physical layer up, isolate one variable at a time, and say when you would escalate. For facilities roles, it might be a UPS alarm or a high-temperature alert in a data hall.
  • Hardware and systems knowledge: IT roles cover server components, drives, memory, power supplies, copper and fiber cabling, and basic Linux and networking. Facilities roles cover electrical distribution, UPS, transfer switches, generators, chilled water, CRAH units, and building management systems.
  • Safety and procedure: lockout/tagout, arc flash boundaries, working from a method of procedure (MOP), and change control. Saying "I would stop and follow the procedure" is a strong answer, not a weak one.
  • Behavioral: "Tell me about a time" questions. Answer with the STAR structure (situation, task, action, result). At Amazon, connect your examples to its published Leadership Principles.

For practice, work through our data center technician interview questions, which include sample answers on safety, power, cooling, and scenarios. Our Incident Response & Troubleshooting course builds the structured approach interviewers listen for.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a degree to work in an Amazon, Google, or Microsoft data center?

Not for most technician roles. Amazon data center technician postings we reviewed list a high school diploma or equivalent plus about a year of hardware troubleshooting or related IT experience. Google lists a bachelor's degree as preferred, alongside equivalent practical experience. Microsoft lists high school, an apprenticeship or vocational qualification, or an equivalent. Engineering roles usually do ask for a degree.

What is a DCEO technician at AWS?

DCEO stands for Data Center Engineering Operations. A Data Center Engineering Operations Technician works on the facility side of an AWS data center: taking operational readings, monitoring maintenance on electrical, mechanical, and fire and life safety equipment, managing contractors on site, and responding to facility events. Postings ask for electrical or mechanical facilities maintenance experience.

Does Microsoft have a data center training program?

Yes. Microsoft runs Datacenter Pathways, which include the Microsoft Datacenter Academy with partner community colleges, a Military Datacenter Pathway for transitioning service members eligible for SkillBridge, a Neurodiversity Datacenter Pathway, and a Communities Datacenter Pathway for seniors and alumni of Historically Black Colleges and Universities. Check Microsoft's Datacenter Pathways page for current details.

How much do Google and Amazon data center technicians make?

We do not quote company pay. Many U.S. postings from these companies include a pay range, and several states require one, so the posting itself is the most reliable source for a specific role and location. For national context on comparable occupations, see the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and our data center technician salary guide.

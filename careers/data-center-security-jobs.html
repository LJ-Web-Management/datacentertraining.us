Data Center Security Jobs: Roles, Skills & Training

Customers trust data centers with equipment and data worth far more than the building. Security officers, supervisors, SOC operators, compliance specialists, and safety professionals protect that trust. Here is what each role does, the licenses and certifications involved, what BLS data says about pay, and how to find openings.

Short answer: Data center security jobs protect the building, the equipment, and the people inside. The main roles are security officer, security supervisor or site security manager, security operations center (SOC) operator, compliance or audit specialist, and environmental, health, and safety (EHS) roles. Officer jobs usually need a high school diploma, a state guard license where required, and a clean background check.

Key takeaways

  • Security officers control access: identity checks, badges, escorts, cameras, patrols, and incident reports. Precision matters more than force.
  • Most states license security guards, and data center employers add their own background checks.
  • "Data center security certification" can mean a credential for people (such as ASIS's PSP) or an audit of the facility (such as SOC 2). This page covers both.
  • BLS reports a median annual wage of $38,020 for security guards in May 2025; supervisory, compliance, and safety roles are benchmarked against other BLS occupations.

What security jobs are there in a data center?

Five groups of roles cover physical security, oversight, and safety. Titles vary by employer, and many officers work for contract security companies assigned to a data center rather than for the operator directly.

A note on "SOC": in physical security it means a security operations center that watches cameras and alarms. In IT it means a cyber security operations center. Postings use both, so read the duties. Compliance teams also use "SOC 2," which is an audit report, not a job.

What does a data center security officer do?

A data center security officer makes sure only authorized people get in, and that they only go where they are allowed. The work is procedural and detailed:

  • Access control: checking government ID against approved access lists, issuing temporary badges, enrolling biometrics, and recovering badges at exit.
  • Anti-tailgating: making sure one badge means one person through each door or mantrap, and challenging anyone who follows.
  • Escorts: staying with visitors and contractors who are not cleared for unescorted access, from lobby to cage and back.
  • CCTV and alarms: watching cameras, responding to door-forced and door-held alarms, and logging every event.
  • Patrols: interior and perimeter rounds, checking doors, fences, loading docks, and generator yards.
  • Deliveries and media: inspecting inbound shipments and verifying that outbound equipment and storage media leave with the right paperwork.
  • Incident reports: clear, factual reports that auditors and investigators can rely on later.

Officers also need to know what to do when the fire alarm sounds or a clean agent suppression system is about to discharge, because they are often the first to direct people out. Do you need a license for data center security jobs?

Usually. According to BLS, most states require security guards to be licensed by the state in which they work, and armed guard positions have more stringent background checks and entry requirements than unarmed ones. Licensing typically involves a background check and state-specified training, and requirements vary widely by state. Check your state's licensing agency before applying.

Data center employers add their own layer: criminal background checks, identity verification, and sometimes drug screening, before you are granted access. Read each posting to see whether a post is armed or unarmed.

Data center safety jobs

Data center safety jobs protect workers rather than assets. They exist on both operating sites and construction projects, and large builds need safety staff for every phase.

  • EHS specialist or manager (operating sites): runs electrical safety and lockout/tagout programs, confined space and hot work permits, contractor safety orientation, incident investigation, and training records. Also manages environmental items such as generator air permits, fuel storage, and refrigerant records. Facility Environmental Compliance covers the environmental side.
  • Site safety manager or coordinator (construction): pre-task planning, fall protection, excavation and electrical hazards, and daily inspections. Many contractors ask workers for OSHA 10-hour or 30-hour Outreach cards, issued through OSHA-authorized trainers.

Safety professionals often pursue credentials from the Board of Certified Safety Professionals (BCSP), such as the Associate Safety Professional (ASP) and Certified Safety Professional (CSP). Our data center safety training guide covers what site safety training includes.

Certifications and audits for facilities

Facilities prove their security to customers through independent audits: SOC 2 reports against the AICPA Trust Services Criteria, ISO/IEC 27001 certification (its Annex A includes physical controls), PCI DSS assessments (Requirement 9 restricts physical access to cardholder data), and HIPAA Security Rule physical safeguards for health data. Security officers generate much of the evidence, such as visitor logs, badge records, and camera retention checks. Security & Compliance for Data Centers explains what each framework asks for.

How much do data center security jobs pay?

BLS does not report data center security pay separately. These comparable BLS occupations show national median annual wages for May 2025:

For security guards, BLS reports the lowest 10 percent earned less than $30,210 and the highest 10 percent more than $58,840. Data center posts may differ from the overall guard market because of site requirements, shift differentials, and supervisory duties. Many states and cities require pay ranges in job postings, so local postings show what specific employers offer.

How to find data center security jobs near me

Data center security jobs follow the data centers, so they are most common in metros with many facilities, but colocation and enterprise sites elsewhere hire officers too. To find openings near you:

  1. Check your state license first

    Find out what your state requires for unarmed security work, and start the process early, since background checks take time.

  2. Search with the right terms

    Use "data center security officer," "security operations center operator," "site security supervisor," and "data center safety" with your city or ZIP code.

  3. Go to the employers directly

    Contract security companies staff many data centers, and operators list security, compliance, and EHS roles on their own careers pages.

  4. Show you understand the environment

    Mention access control systems, escort procedures, report writing, and any audit experience on your resume.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a license to work data center security?

In most states, yes. BLS notes that most states require security guards to be licensed by the state in which they work, and armed positions have stricter background checks and entry requirements. Requirements differ by state, so check your state licensing agency. Employers also run their own background checks before granting data center access.

Is data center security a good entry-level job?

It is one of the more accessible ways into the data center industry, because many officer roles need a high school diploma, a state guard license, and a clean background rather than technical experience. You learn how the site works, meet the facilities and IT teams, and can move toward security supervision, compliance, or other roles with added training.

What certifications help for data center security jobs?

For officers, a state guard license and any employer-required training come first. For supervisors and managers, ASIS International offers the Associate Protection Professional (APP), Physical Security Professional (PSP), and Certified Protection Professional (CPP). Compliance and cyber roles look at credentials such as ISACA's CISA and ISC2's CISSP. Check each issuer for current requirements.

What are data center safety jobs?

Safety jobs at data centers include EHS specialists and managers at operating sites and safety coordinators and site safety managers on construction projects. They run electrical safety, lockout/tagout, and contractor safety programs, investigate incidents, track training, and manage environmental permits for generators and fuel storage.

How do I find data center security jobs near me?

Search job boards for data center security officer, security operations center operator, and site security supervisor with your city or ZIP code. Check the careers pages of contract security companies and of data center operators in your area, and make sure you hold, or can get, the guard license your state requires before you apply.

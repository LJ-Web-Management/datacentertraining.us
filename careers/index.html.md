Why Data Centers

## An Industry That Is Growing and Hiring

Data centers are using more power every year, and the operators who run them say finding and keeping skilled people is hard. That combination is good news for anyone willing to learn the work.

4.4%

of total U.S. electricity was used by data centers in 2023 (about 176 TWh). DOE and LBNL project about 6.7% to 12% by 2028 (325 to 580 TWh).

Source: [U.S. DOE / Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers)

data center operators report difficulty retaining staff, finding qualified candidates, or both.

Source: [Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey 2025](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/uptime-institute-global-data-center-survey-2025)

Career Ladder

## How a Facilities Career Typically Grows

Titles vary by employer, but most critical facilities careers climb a ladder like this one. Each step adds responsibility for more equipment, more people, or more sites.

1. **Entry technician**Rounds and readings, preventive maintenance support, vendor escorts, and learning the site's procedures.
2. **Technician II / III**Leads maintenance tasks, performs switching under written procedures, troubleshoots, and trains newer techs.
3. **Lead or shift supervisor**Runs a shift, assigns work, owns alarm response, and signs off on shift turnover.
4. **Chief engineer or facility manager**Owns the maintenance program, vendors, budget, and the site's risk decisions.
5. **Director of critical facilities**Leads several sites or a region: standards, staffing, and capital planning.

The ladder has side doors, too. Experienced technicians often move into [commissioning](https://datacentertraining.us/careers/data-center-jobs.html#commissioning-agent), [controls](https://datacentertraining.us/careers/data-center-jobs.html#controls-technician), [capacity planning](https://datacentertraining.us/careers/data-center-jobs.html#capacity-planner), or [safety and compliance](https://datacentertraining.us/careers/data-center-jobs.html#ehs-manager). IT-side careers follow a similar path from technician to lead to data center operations manager.

Employers

## Where You Could Work

The same skills travel across very different employers. Each one offers a different pace, team size, and culture.

### Colocation providers

Lease space, power, and cooling to many customers in one building. Expect customer tours, remote hands requests, and strict service level agreements.

### Hyperscale and cloud

Large campuses run by cloud and internet companies. Highly standardized designs and procedures, large teams, and constant expansion.

### Enterprise data centers

Banks, hospitals, retailers, and universities that run their own facilities. Smaller teams mean broader roles and more variety.

### Construction and commissioning firms

General, electrical, and mechanical contractors and commissioning agents who build and test new capacity, often on large campus projects.

### Manufacturers and service vendors

UPS, generator, switchgear, and cooling manufacturers, plus the field service teams and maintenance contractors that support many sites.

### Government and public sector

Federal, state, military, and research facilities. Some roles require a security clearance or background investigation.

Skills

## What Employers Look For

Hiring managers ask about these four areas in almost every interview, whatever the title on the job post.

Safety

Safety habits

- Lockout/tagout and verifying zero energy
- NFPA 70E arc flash and shock awareness
- Right PPE for the task
- Using stop-work authority

Technical

Fundamentals

- Electrical theory and meter use
- Refrigeration cycle and chilled water
- Reading one-lines and schematics
- Basic networking and cabling

Process

Procedural discipline

- Following MOPs, SOPs, and EOPs step by step
- Change management
- Work orders and maintenance records
- Accurate logs and readings

People

Communication

- Clear shift turnovers
- Knowing when and how to escalate
- Staying calm during alarms
- Professional with customers and vendors

FAQ

## Data Center Career Questions

### Do I need a college degree to work in a data center?

Usually not for technician and operations roles. Employers hiring data center technicians, critical facilities technicians, electricians, HVAC technicians, and security officers tend to value trade training, licenses where the work requires them, military experience, or IT support experience. Design engineering roles typically require an engineering degree, and some management roles prefer one.

### How much do data center jobs pay?

Pay varies by region, shift, role, and experience, so we do not quote figures. For national pay data on related occupations, see the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook pages for [electricians](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/electricians.htm) and for [heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/heating-air-conditioning-and-refrigeration-mechanics-and-installers.htm). Ask each employer how shift differentials and on-call duty are handled.

### Is data center work shift work?

Many operations roles are. Facilities run around the clock, so technicians, NOC staff, and security officers often work rotating 8-hour or 12-hour shifts that include nights, weekends, and holidays. Engineering, project, and management roles are more often on day schedules, usually with on-call duty.

### Will your courses get me a job or a certification?

No course can promise a job. Our self-paced courses award a certificate of completion, which shows an employer you studied a topic. They are not a license or a third-party certification. Many learners use them to build the vocabulary and safety foundations employers look for, then add hands-on experience and any credentials the role requires.

### What is the best way into the industry with no experience?

Common entry points are data center technician roles for people with IT support experience, security officer roles, and junior facilities technician roles for people with a mechanical or electrical background. Learning how power, cooling, and safety work before you apply helps you speak the language in an interview.

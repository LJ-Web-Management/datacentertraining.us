# How Many Jobs Does a Data Center Create?

Careers · Industry Facts

The honest answer depends on which jobs you count: construction or operations, on-site or across the economy. Here is what the government reviews, industry studies, and company announcements actually say, and who paid for each.

**Short answer:** How many jobs a data center creates depends on the phase. Building one large facility puts roughly 1,000 to 2,000 construction workers on site at peak, and the largest AI campuses report several thousand. Once running, a typical data center has about 50 full-time workers, according to Virginia's legislative auditors, while big multi-building campuses report from about 100 to 1,000 permanent jobs.

Key takeaways

- Construction is where most data center jobs are. In Virginia, the construction phase accounted for about 80 percent of the industry's total annual job impact.
- Permanent on-site headcount is small for the size of the building, and about half of it may be contractors.
- Big national totals (millions of jobs) include indirect and induced jobs estimated with economic models, and use broad definitions of the industry.
- Many impact studies are paid for by industry groups. Read who funded a study, and how it counts jobs, before you quote it.

## How many jobs does a data center create during construction?

A lot, for a while. Virginia's Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission (JLARC), the research arm of the state legislature, reported in 2024 that building an individual data center usually takes about 12 to 18 months, that a full campus can take five or more years, and that roughly 1,500 workers are on site at the height of construction.

Company figures line up with that. Meta's own location list shows 1,000 to 2,000 skilled trade workers on site at peak construction for most of its U.S. campuses. AI campuses are bigger: Meta lists more than 4,000 at peak for El Paso and more than 7,500 for Richland Parish, Louisiana, and Microsoft says its Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin, AI datacenter employed more than 3,000 construction workers at daily peak.

These are real jobs with real paychecks, but they are temporary at any one site. Trades workers often follow the work from building to building on a campus, and from one campus to the next. That is why JLARC concluded that continued growth would be needed to sustain the same level of economic impact.

## How many permanent jobs does a data center create?

Far fewer. JLARC reported that data center representatives described a typical 250,000-square-foot facility as having about 50 full-time workers: roughly one worker per 5,000 square feet, compared with one per 650 square feet in some distribution centers. About half are direct employees, such as facility managers, engineers, data technicians, and maintenance staff. The other half are contractors who work full time at the site, including electricians, pipefitters, and security personnel.

Across Virginia, JLARC estimated that data center employees and full-time contractors held more than 8,000 full-time jobs in fiscal year 2023, and that the industry added more than 800 such jobs that year.

Large campuses, which contain several buildings, publish larger permanent numbers. Meta lists roughly 100 to 400-plus operational jobs supported at most of its U.S. campuses and about 1,000 for its Louisiana campus. Microsoft expects around 500 full-time employees in Mount Pleasant, growing to around 800. Indiana said Google's Fort Wayne data center is expected to support up to 200 jobs.

## Why is permanent headcount so small?

Because a data center is built to hold equipment, not people. A few reasons stand out:

- **Automation and remote monitoring.** Building management and monitoring systems watch thousands of points around the clock, so a small on-site team can respond to alarms.
- **Shift coverage, not crowds.** Sites run 24/7, but each shift needs a core team rather than a large workforce. See [how data center operations work](https://datacentertraining.us/learn/data-center-operations.html).
- **Vendors and contractors.** Specialized work, such as UPS, generator, and chiller service, is often done by manufacturer technicians and contractors who visit many sites.
- **Size is measured in megawatts.** A building can grow in power and equipment without adding many people, which is why jobs per square foot look low.

Small does not mean unimportant. These teams keep critical systems running, and JLARC found that both operations and construction workers earn average or above-average wages.

## What are indirect and induced jobs?

Most big job numbers you see for data centers include jobs away from the site. Economic impact studies split them into three groups:

- **Direct jobs:** people employed by the data center industry itself (and, in construction-phase estimates, the construction workers).
- **Indirect jobs:** jobs at suppliers, such as materials, equipment, utilities, and professional services.
- **Induced jobs:** jobs supported when direct and supplier workers spend their pay at local businesses, such as restaurants and stores.

These indirect and induced figures are estimates from input-output models such as IMPLAN or REMI, not head counts. They tell you how much activity a sector supports, not how many people are on a payroll at the site. JLARC's appendix cautioned that the standard IMPLAN sector for data centers does not match real data center spending well, for example underweighting electricity and overweighting employee compensation, which may inflate induced impacts.

### How to read these numbers

1. **Check the definition of "direct."** PwC's 2026 study uses an expanded, function-based definition that counts data center services wherever they are produced, including IT services establishments outside the core data center industry code. That is much broader than the people working inside data center buildings.
2. **Separate construction from operations.** JLARC shows most of the impact comes from construction, which depends on continued building.
3. **Remember that "supported" is not "created."** Model-based totals estimate activity linked to the sector, statewide or nationally, not new jobs in the host town.
4. **Look at who paid.** Industry-funded studies are not necessarily wrong, but they are written to show benefits. Government reviews like JLARC's also weigh costs, such as energy and land use.

## What kinds of jobs does a data center create?

JLARC lists the construction occupations as site developers and surveyors, equipment operators for land clearing and leveling, workers who erect steel frames and concrete walls, electricians installing cabling, equipment, and generators, and pipefitters and HVAC technicians installing piping and cooling equipment. Commissioning agents and safety staff work alongside them near the end.

Operations jobs include facility managers, engineers, data center technicians, critical facilities and maintenance technicians, electricians, controls technicians, and security officers. Indiana's announcement for Google's Fort Wayne site listed facilities roles, data center technicians, and support services such as security, catering, and grounds keeping. ## Frequently asked questions

### How many permanent jobs does a data center create?

Fewer than most people expect. Virginia's Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission found that a typical 250,000-square-foot data center has about 50 full-time workers, about half of them contractors. Large multi-building campuses publish bigger numbers: Meta lists roughly 100 to 400 operational jobs at most of its U.S. campuses and about 1,000 at its largest, in Louisiana.

### How many jobs does a data center provide during construction?

Many more than in operations. Virginia's JLARC reported about 1,500 workers on site at the height of construction for a data center facility. Meta lists 1,000 to 2,000 skilled trade workers at peak for most of its campuses, and the largest AI campuses report several thousand, such as more than 7,500 at Meta's Richland Parish, Louisiana, site.

### How long do data center construction jobs last?

JLARC reports that one data center building usually takes about 12 to 18 months to build, and a full campus can take five or more years. Individual trades may be on site for only part of that time, but workers often move from building to building on a campus, or from one campus to the next.

### Why do data centers create so few permanent jobs?

A data center is full of equipment, not people. Systems are monitored remotely and run around the clock with small shift teams, and specialized maintenance is often done by visiting vendors and contractors. JLARC compared one worker per 5,000 square feet in a typical data center with one per 650 square feet in some distribution centers.

### Who pays for data center economic impact studies?

Many are commissioned by industry groups. The national PwC studies were commissioned by the Data Center Coalition, the industry's trade association, and the U.S. Chamber of Commerce has published its own analyses. Government reviews, such as Virginia's 2024 JLARC study, are independent of the industry. Check who paid for a study and how it defines direct jobs before comparing numbers.

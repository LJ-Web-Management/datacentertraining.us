**Short answer:** AI data center jobs are the construction, electrical, mechanical, commissioning, and operations roles needed to build and run campuses full of GPU servers. The biggest hiring wave is in the skilled trades, mostly through contractors. Each finished campus then needs a smaller permanent team of critical facilities technicians, data center technicians, network staff, and security.

Key takeaways

- AI racks draw far more power than traditional racks, so AI campuses rely on liquid cooling, heavier electrical distribution, and very large utility connections.
- Construction employs thousands of tradespeople per campus. Permanent operations teams are measured in hundreds.
- Electricians, pipefitters, commissioning agents, and critical facilities technicians are the roles most tied to AI-specific work.
- Liquid cooling, high-power distribution, arc flash safety, and commissioning are the skills that set candidates apart.

## What is different about an AI data center?

Three things: power density, liquid cooling, and scale. Together they change what gets built and who builds and runs it.

- **Power density.** Traditional enterprise racks commonly draw single-digit kilowatts. A reference design that Vertiv co-developed with NVIDIA for the GB200 NVL72 platform supports up to 132 kW per rack. That pushes facilities toward higher-voltage distribution, overhead busway, and larger switchgear and transformers.
- **Liquid cooling.** Air alone cannot remove that much heat from a rack, so AI halls bring liquid to the chip through coolant distribution units (CDUs). Microsoft says more than 90 percent of its Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin, AI datacenter relies on a closed-loop liquid cooling system, and the planned Lighthouse campus in Port Washington, Wisconsin, is designed around closed-loop liquid cooling as well.
- **Scale.** Single AI campuses are now planned at around a gigawatt. The Abilene, Texas, Stargate campus has about 1.2 GW of planned power capacity, and the Lighthouse campus is planned at close to a gigawatt. For context, the U.S. Department of Energy reports that data centers used about 4.4 percent of U.S. electricity in 2023 and could use about 6.7 to 12 percent by 2028.

Hiring comes from four kinds of employers: the companies that will use the capacity (hyperscalers and AI labs), developers and AI cloud providers that build and lease it, the general and trade contractors that construct it, and facilities service firms that help run it. ## AI data center job families

The job families are the same as in any data center. What changes is the mix, the scale, and the systems people work on.

**Job families on an AI data center campus**

| Job family | What they do on an AI campus | Typical employer | Phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Electricians | Install medium-voltage gear, transformers, switchgear, busway, and branch circuits; terminate and test | Electrical contractors; later the operator | Construction, then operations |
| Pipefitters and plumbers | Build chilled water and liquid cooling loops, CDU connections, and process piping; pressure test and flush | Mechanical contractors | Construction |
| Other construction trades | Carpenters, ironworkers, welders, fiber technicians, and equipment operators who build the shell and pathways | General and trade contractors | Construction |
| Commissioning agents | Run factory, installation, start-up, functional, and integrated systems tests before handover | Commissioning firms, owners | Late construction |
| Critical facilities technicians | Operate and maintain power and cooling plants, including CDUs and liquid loops; respond to alarms | Operators, facilities service firms | Operations |
| Data center technicians | Install, cable, and repair GPU servers and high-speed network gear | Operators, AI clouds | Operations |
| Network and fiber technicians | Install and test the dense fiber that links GPU clusters | Operators, contractors | Both |
| EHS and security | Run site safety for large crews; control access to the campus and halls | Owners, contractors, security firms | Both |

The trades named by the companies themselves line up with this list. Microsoft lists electricians, plumbers and pipefitters, carpenters, and structural iron and steel workers on its Wisconsin site, and Meta's America's Workforce Academy names fiber technicians, welders, plumbers, electricians, and mechanics among the trades it is training for. For the role-by-role view, see our [guide to data center jobs](https://datacentertraining.us/careers/data-center-jobs.html).

## Are electricians in demand for AI data centers?

Yes. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects employment of electricians to grow 9 percent from 2025 to 2035, with about 72,700 openings a year on average, and it names AI and data center demand among the reasons. The BLS reports a median annual wage for electricians of $63,190 in May 2025. BLS does not track data center electricians separately, so treat that as the figure for the comparable national occupation; pay on a given project depends on region, union or open shop, overtime, and per diem.

For comparison, BLS reports a median annual wage of $63,800 in May 2025 for plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters, with employment projected to grow 7 percent from 2025 to 2035. Many employers must also post pay ranges in job ads under state laws, so check each posting. Our [data center electrician guide](https://datacentertraining.us/careers/data-center-electrician.html) covers licensing and the path in.

## What skills matter more at AI data centers?

The fundamentals stay the same: safety, procedures, power, and cooling. These skills simply carry more weight at AI sites:

- **Liquid cooling.** CDUs, manifolds, quick disconnects, water treatment and chemistry, filtration, leak detection, and pressure testing. [Cooling Systems Design & Optimization](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/cooling-systems-design-optimization.html) builds the foundation.
- **High-power electrical distribution.** Medium-voltage switchgear, large transformers, busway, and rack power shelves. More power in a smaller space can mean higher available fault current, so arc flash studies and labels matter even more. See [Power Systems & Electrical Fundamentals](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/power-systems-electrical-fundamentals.html) and [Data Center Arc Flash Safety](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/data-center-arc-flash-safety.html).
- **Commissioning.** AI campuses hand over building after building, each tested from factory witness through integrated systems testing. People who can read sequences of operation and run test scripts are in demand. Start with [Infrastructure Commissioning & Startup](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/infrastructure-commissioning-startup.html).
- **Mechanical maintenance.** Pumps, valves, heat exchangers, and chillers that now sit directly in the critical path.
- **Capacity planning.** Tracking power, cooling, and space as GPU deployments arrive in large blocks.
- **Working safely on a live construction site.** Many campuses energize and operate early buildings while later ones are still under construction, so crews must manage the boundary between construction and live critical systems.

## How is AI used in data center operations?

AI tools help operators tune cooling and spot problems early, but people still run the site. In 2016 Google reported that applying DeepMind machine learning in its data centers cut the energy used for cooling by 40 percent, equal to a 15 percent reduction in overall PUE overhead. Today, operators also use analytics in monitoring platforms to flag unusual readings and predict maintenance needs.

For technicians, that means more data and better alerts, not fewer jobs. Someone still has to verify the reading, follow the method of procedure, open the panel safely, and approve the change. Understanding monitoring systems, from the BMS to DCIM, helps you work with these tools; see [energy efficiency and PUE](https://datacentertraining.us/learn/energy-efficiency-pue.html) for how cooling efficiency is measured.

## How to prepare for an AI data center job

1. ### Pick your phase

   Construction pays off for licensed trades and people willing to travel between projects. Operations suits those who want a long-term site role. Many people start in construction and move into operations at the same campus.
2. ### Get the credential the work requires

   Electricians need the license their state requires, usually after a registered apprenticeship. Technicians who open refrigerant circuits need EPA Section 608 certification. OSHA 10 and OSHA 30 Outreach cards, issued through OSHA-authorized trainers, are common on construction sites. Programs such as Meta's America's Workforce Academy award NCCER credentials.
3. ### Learn the AI-specific systems

   Study liquid cooling, high-power distribution, and commissioning, the areas that differ most from older data halls.
4. ### Build safety knowledge

   NFPA 70E awareness, lockout/tagout, and arc flash basics are expected before you set foot in an electrical room. Our courses provide awareness training; your employer still qualifies you for specific tasks.
5. ### Search the right employers

   For construction, contact the electrical and mechanical contractors and union halls staffing the project. For operations, search owners' and operators' careers sites by location.

## Frequently asked questions

### What jobs are there in AI data centers?

During construction, the largest group is the skilled trades: electricians, pipefitters and plumbers, carpenters, ironworkers, fiber technicians, and welders, plus commissioning agents and safety staff. Once a campus is running, it needs critical facilities technicians for power and cooling, data center technicians who install and repair GPU servers, network and fiber technicians, security officers, and managers.

### Do AI data centers create permanent jobs?

Yes, but far fewer than during construction. Microsoft says its Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin, AI datacenter employed more than 3,000 construction workers at daily peak and expects around 500 full-time employees, growing to around 800. Meta lists about 1,000 operational jobs for its Richland Parish, Louisiana, campus versus more than 7,500 trade workers at peak construction.

### What AI data center jobs are in Abilene, Texas?

Abilene hosts the flagship Stargate campus, developed by Crusoe and leased to Oracle and OpenAI. Construction drew thousands of electricians and other tradespeople through contractors. For operations, Oracle has posted data center support services technician roles in Abilene, including military pathway programs, and nearby Shackelford County has Oracle and Vantage facilities postings.

### How do I become an AI data center electrician?

Most electricians on these projects come up through a registered apprenticeship and hold the license their state requires. Contractors building AI campuses hire journeyworkers and apprentices, and some programs, such as Meta's America's Workforce Academy, offer fast-track trades training. Learning medium-voltage distribution, busway, arc flash safety, and commissioning makes you more useful on a data center job site.

### Is AI used to run data centers?

Yes, in specific tasks. Google reported in 2016 that DeepMind machine learning cut the energy used for cooling in its data centers by 40 percent. Operators also use analytics to spot anomalies in monitoring data. People still approve changes, perform maintenance, follow procedures, and respond to alarms, so AI tools add to technician work rather than replace it.

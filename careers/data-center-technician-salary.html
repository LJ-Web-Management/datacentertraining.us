Data Center Technician Salary: What the Job Pays and Why

Data center tech salary numbers online disagree, and most are self-reported. This guide uses only U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data for comparable occupations, explains what actually moves data center pay, and shows you how to find real employer ranges.

Short answer: Data center technician salary is not tracked as its own occupation by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. The closest comparable, computer support specialists, had a median annual wage of $62,890 in May 2025, and computer network support specialists had $76,220. Real pay depends on level, shift, region, and whether the role is IT or facilities, and many postings list a range.

Key takeaways

  • The BLS has no "data center technician" occupation, so use comparable occupations as benchmarks, not as exact pay.
  • Level (L1 to L3), shift differentials, overtime, region, and licenses move pay more than the job title does.
  • Pay transparency laws in several states mean many postings now show a real range. That is the best source for a specific employer.
  • The surest way to earn more is to add skills that move you up a level or across into facilities, networking, or management.

How much does a data center technician make?

Data center technician pay typically falls in the same range as comparable IT support and network support work, before shift differentials and overtime. Using BLS data, computer support specialists had a median annual wage of $62,890 in May 2025, and computer network support specialists, whose work is closer to cabling and network troubleshooting, had a median of $76,220.

Those are national medians, meaning half of workers in the occupation earned more and half earned less. They are not data center technician pay. They are the best public, consistently measured benchmarks available, and they are far more reliable than self-reported salary sites. For a specific employer, read its posted range (see how to find real pay ranges).

If you are new to the role itself, start with our data center technician job guide, which covers duties, requirements, and how to get hired.

What drives data center pay?

Six factors explain most of the difference between two data center job offers: level, shift, region, IT versus facilities, licenses, and overtime or on-call pay.

Factors that move data center technician pay
FactorHow it affects pay
Level (L1, L2, L3)Higher levels take on harder troubleshooting, projects, and mentoring, and are posted at higher ranges. See the career path guide.
Shift differentialsMany employers add a differential for nights, weekends, or both. Ask whether the posted range includes it.
RegionLocal labor markets and cost of living shape ranges. The same title can be posted at very different ranges in different metro areas.
IT side vs. facilities sideFacilities roles often draw on licensed trades. BLS medians show the gap: stationary engineers and boiler operators at $78,620 versus computer support specialists at $62,890 (May 2025).
Licenses and credentialsAn electrician license or EPA Section 608 certification can be required for facilities roles and opens jobs closed to unlicensed candidates.
Overtime and on-callUnder the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, covered non-exempt employees earn at least one and a half times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Some 12-hour rotations alternate shorter and longer weeks, so the longer weeks can include overtime, depending on how the employer defines the workweek.

Employer type matters too. Hyperscale operators, colocation providers, enterprises, and contractors structure pay differently, with different mixes of base pay, bonus, equity, and benefits. Compare total compensation, not only the base number.

Entry-level vs. L3 data center technician salary

Entry-level data center technicians are usually hired toward the low end of a posted range, and L3 technicians toward the high end or into a higher range. The BLS does not publish pay by technician level, so use these reference points:

  • Entry level (L1, technician I): computer user support specialists, median $61,860 (May 2025), is the closest national benchmark for first-job hardware and support work.
  • Mid level (L2): computer support specialists overall, median $62,890, and telecommunications technicians, median $66,200 (May 2025), bracket technicians with solid cabling and break-fix experience.
  • Senior level (L3, technician III): computer network support specialists, median $76,220 (May 2025), is closer to the network troubleshooting and project work L3 technicians do.

Treat these as reference points, not promises. Employers define levels differently, and an L3 at one company may be an L2 at another. Our entry-level data center jobs guide covers what first roles ask for.

How to find real data center pay ranges

The most reliable pay number for a specific job is the range in the employer's own posting. Pay transparency laws in several states now require many employers to include a wage or salary range in job postings, including Colorado, California, Washington, New York, and Illinois. Each state sets its own rules about which employers and jobs are covered, so check the state agency for details.

  1. Search the employer's own career site

    Postings on company career sites are the original source. Third-party job boards sometimes drop or alter the pay section.

  2. Compare the same title, level, and location

    A "Data Center Technician II" range in one metro area does not tell you much about an L1 role in another. Line up like with like.

  3. Read what the range includes

    Some ranges are base pay only. Look for separate mentions of shift differentials, bonus, equity, and benefits.

  4. Ask the recruiter directly

    Ask where in the range offers usually land for your experience, how differentials work, and what the overtime pattern looks like on your rotation.

Google, Amazon (AWS), Microsoft, and Oracle data center technician salary

We do not publish company-specific pay figures, because they change often and differ by level, location, and role. You can research them directly and accurately: the large cloud companies post their U.S. data center openings on their own career sites, and postings for jobs in pay-transparency states generally include a pay range.

  • Search the official sites: Google Careers, Amazon Jobs (which includes AWS), Microsoft Careers, and Oracle Careers.
  • Search the titles each company uses, such as data center technician, data center operations technician, or data center inventory and logistics roles, and note the level in the title.
  • Open several postings in different states. Ranges for the same job often differ by location.
  • Read the compensation section in full. Large employers often describe bonus, stock, and benefits separately from base pay.

Data center jobs salary by role

Pay for other data center roles follows their trade or profession. Here is how each one compares, using BLS medians for May 2025, with a link to the full role guide.

Data center electrician salary

The BLS reports a median annual wage of $63,190 for electricians in May 2025. The lowest 10 percent earned less than $42,640 and the highest 10 percent more than $108,510, and the BLS notes that overtime is common, especially during scheduled maintenance. Demand looks solid: the BLS projects electrician employment to grow 9 percent from 2025 to 2035 and notes that demand for data centers is expected to contribute to rising electricity demand. See data center electrician.

Data center engineer salary

"Data center engineer" covers several professions. Electrical engineers had a median of $120,630 and mechanical engineers $104,110 (May 2025). Engineers on the IT side are closer to network and computer systems administrators, at $99,130 (May 2025). See data center engineer.

Data center manager, operations manager, and project manager salary

Facilities managers had a median of $106,660 (May 2025) and are the closest match for data center facility and operations managers. Senior IT infrastructure leaders compare more closely with computer and information systems managers, at $175,140 (May 2025). Data center project managers compare with project management specialists, at $102,320 (May 2025). See data center facility manager.

Data center HVAC technician salary

Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers had a median of $61,010 (May 2025). Technicians who handle refrigerants need EPA Section 608 certification. See data center HVAC technician.

Critical facilities technician salary

Critical facilities work blends electrical, mechanical, and building operations. Stationary engineers and boiler operators, at $78,620 (May 2025), is a reasonable comparable, alongside electricians and HVAC mechanics. See critical facilities technician.

How to raise your data center pay

You raise your pay by becoming worth a higher level or a better-paid role. In practice that means adding skills employers pay for, proving them, and asking for the move.

  • Master the skills of the next level. Fiber testing, network troubleshooting, and leading small deployments are what usually separate L1 from L2 and L3.
  • Learn power and cooling. IT technicians who understand UPS systems, generators, and cooling are candidates for facilities, capacity, and operations roles. Start with Power Systems & Electrical Fundamentals.
  • Earn a credential or license where it matters. IT credentials from organizations such as CompTIA and BICSI, an electrician license from your state, or EPA Section 608 certification each open doors. See data center certifications.
  • Take on leadership. Shift lead, trainer, and procedure-writer roles build the record you need for a lead or supervisor posting. Data Center Operations Management covers change, incident, and shift management.
  • Document your training. List completed courses honestly as training on your resume. A certificate of completion shows initiative, but it is not a certification or license.
  • Compare offers on total compensation. A slightly lower base with a night differential, bonus, and better benefits can be worth more.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an entry-level data center technician make?

The BLS does not publish entry-level pay for data center technicians, because it does not track the role as a separate occupation. The closest benchmark is computer user support specialists, with a median annual wage of $61,860 in May 2025. New technicians are usually hired toward the lower end of an employer's posted range, so read the range in each posting and ask how shift differentials apply.

Do data center technicians get paid more for night shifts?

Many employers pay a shift differential for nights, weekends, or both, but it is not universal and the amount varies. Ask the recruiter whether the posted range includes differentials, how they are calculated, and whether the schedule includes any regular overtime hours.

How much do data center electricians make?

The BLS does not separate data center electricians from other electricians. For all electricians, the median annual wage was $63,190 in May 2025, with the lowest 10 percent earning less than $42,640 and the highest 10 percent earning more than $108,510. The BLS also notes that overtime is common for electricians.

Do Google, Amazon, Microsoft, or Oracle pay data center technicians more?

We do not publish company-specific pay. The reliable way to compare is to read each company's own job postings for the same title, level, and location. Postings for roles in states with pay transparency laws usually include a pay range, and many also describe bonus, equity, and benefits.

What is the salary of an L3 data center technician?

There is no BLS figure for technician levels. L3 roles usually carry more complex troubleshooting, project, and mentoring work, so they are posted at higher ranges than L1 roles at the same employer. Compare postings that name the level, and use the BLS median for computer network support specialists, $76,220 in May 2025, as a rough national reference point.

Data Center Facility Manager & Operations Manager

Managers in a data center own the outcomes: uptime, safety, cost, and customer trust. Here is what facility managers and operations managers each own, the discipline of data center operations management, the certifications managers pursue, and what federal data says about pay.

Short answer: A data center facility manager owns the building's critical infrastructure: the maintenance program, vendors, budget, and technical risk decisions. A data center operations manager runs day-to-day operations across shifts: staffing, change and incident management, and service levels. Many sites combine the two. Most managers come up from chief engineer, senior technician, or shift supervisor roles.

Key takeaways

  • Both roles are accountable for uptime. The facility manager focuses on the infrastructure; the operations manager focuses on people, process, and service.
  • Data center operations management is a discipline of its own: maintenance, change, incident, capacity, vendor, and compliance management working as one system.
  • Management certifications are optional and come from several issuers, including CNet Training, EPI, Uptime Institute, and IFMA.
  • The BLS benchmark is its facilities managers occupation, with a median annual wage of $106,660 in May 2025.

What does a data center facility manager do?

A data center facility manager is responsible for the critical infrastructure and the team that maintains it. Their core question is: can this building keep the IT load running through failures and maintenance, safely and at a reasonable cost?

  • Maintenance program: sets preventive maintenance scopes and frequencies, watches completion and backlog, and decides what can wait.
  • Risk decisions: approves methods of procedure (MOPs) for work that reduces redundancy, and says no when the risk is not justified.
  • Vendors: manages service contracts for UPS, generators, chillers, switchgear, and fire systems, and holds vendors to their scope.
  • Budget and capital: plans operating spend and equipment replacement, and builds the case for upgrades.
  • People: hires, trains, and schedules technicians and develops future leads.

BLS describes facilities managers in general as overseeing buildings, grounds, equipment, and supplies, covering operations, maintenance, and project management. A data center adds an unusual level of redundancy, documentation, and consequence to that job.

What does a data center operations manager do?

A data center operations manager makes sure the site delivers what customers or internal users expect, every shift. At colocation providers the role often spans facilities, remote hands, and security; at enterprise sites it may also cover IT operations.

  • Runs shift schedules and staffing, including coverage for nights, holidays, and sick calls.
  • Chairs change review meetings and makes sure approved work follows the plan.
  • Leads incident response and post-incident reviews, and turns lessons into procedure changes.
  • Tracks service level agreement (SLA) performance and customer escalations.
  • Keeps documentation, training records, and audit evidence current.

Searches for "data center manager jobs" return both titles, plus IT-side roles such as data center infrastructure manager. Read the duties to see which kind of manager the employer wants.

What is data center operations management?

Data center operations management is the set of processes that keeps a facility running predictably: who does what, how work is approved, and how the site learns from mistakes. Strong programs connect these pieces:

  1. Maintenance managementPM schedules in a computerized maintenance management system (CMMS), corrective work, and backlog control.
  2. Change managementRisk-ranked change requests, MOPs, approvals, and maintenance windows.
  3. Incident managementEmergency procedures, escalation paths, communication, and root cause reviews.
  4. Capacity managementTracking power, cooling, and space so redundancy holds as load grows.
  5. Vendor managementScopes, performance, site rules, and escorts for every contractor.
  6. Knowledge and trainingCurrent drawings, procedures, and role-based training records.

Uptime Institute assesses this side of a facility through its Operational Sustainability certification, separate from its Tier design and construction certifications. The data center operations guide covers these processes in more depth, and Data Center Operations Management turns them into a working framework.

Staffing is the hard part

In the Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey 2025, nearly two-thirds of operators reported difficulty retaining staff, finding qualified candidates, or both. Managers who build training programs and career ladders have an advantage.

Skills data center managers need

  • Technical depth: enough knowledge of power, cooling, and controls to challenge a bad plan and understand an incident report.
  • Risk judgment: weighing a maintenance task's benefit against the chance it drops load.
  • Financial skills: operating budgets, capital requests, and vendor contract terms.
  • Leadership: hiring, coaching, scheduling, and holding standards on night shift as well as day shift.
  • Continuity planning: disaster recovery and business continuity plans that hold up in drills. See Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity.
  • Capacity forecasting: knowing when the site runs out of power or cooling. Capacity Planning & Forecasting covers the methods.
  • Communication: clear incident updates for customers and executives, and plain procedures for technicians.

How do you become a data center manager?

Most get there by growing through operations roles and taking on management work before the title arrives.

  1. Master the systems

    Spend years as a critical facilities technician, electrician, HVAC technician, or NOC lead. Managers who know the equipment earn their team's trust.

  2. Lead a shift

    Become a lead technician or shift supervisor. You learn scheduling, first response, and how to coach people.

  3. Own a program

    Volunteer for the jobs managers do: write MOPs, run the PM program, manage a vendor, lead post-incident reviews. Facility Management Fundamentals fills in budgeting and planning.

  4. Step into chief engineer or operations supervisor

    These roles carry technical accountability for the whole site and are the usual last step before manager.

  5. Add a credential if it fills a gap

    A management certification or degree can help, especially for regional roles, but experience matters most.

Our critical facilities technician guide covers the first rungs of this ladder.

Data center management certification options

No license is required to manage a data center. Some managers pursue a data center manager certification to structure their learning or strengthen a promotion case. Options people commonly consider, as described by their issuers:

Data center manager salary: BLS benchmarks

BLS does not publish pay for data center managers specifically. Its facilities managers occupation had a median annual wage of $106,660 in May 2025; the lowest 10 percent earned less than $64,480 and the highest 10 percent earned more than $176,120. For data center managers on the IT side, the comparable BLS occupation is computer and information systems managers, with a median annual wage of $175,140 in May 2025.

Actual pay depends on site size, region, scope, and on-call duties. Many states and cities require pay ranges in job postings, so local data center manager jobs are the best guide to a specific employer. See our data center salary guide for other roles.

Frequently asked questions

Is a data center operations manager the same as a facility manager?

They overlap but are not the same. A facility manager owns the building's critical infrastructure: maintenance, vendors, capital, and technical risk. An operations manager owns day-to-day service delivery: shifts, change and incident management, customer requests, and service levels. At smaller sites one person often holds both jobs.

Do you need a degree to become a data center manager?

Not always. Many data center facility managers rose from technician, chief engineer, or military leadership roles, and postings often accept equivalent experience. Some employers prefer or require a degree in engineering, facilities management, or business, especially for regional and director roles. Check each posting's requirements.

What is the best data center management certification?

There is no single standard. Data center specific options include CNet Training's Certified Data Centre Management Professional (CDCMP), EPI's Certified Data Centre Facilities Operations Manager (CDFOM), and Uptime Institute's accreditations. Facility managers also pursue IFMA's FMP or CFM. Pick the one that matches the gaps in your experience and check each issuer for current details.

How do technicians move into data center management?

Usually one step at a time: lead technician or shift supervisor, then chief engineer or operations supervisor, then manager. Volunteer to write MOPs, lead incident reviews, manage a vendor, and help with the budget. Those are the tasks managers do every day, and they show you can do the job before you have the title.

What KPIs do data center operations managers track?

Common measures include availability and customer-impacting incidents, preventive maintenance completion and backlog, change success rate, alarm and response metrics, power usage effectiveness (PUE), capacity used versus available for power, cooling, and space, safety incidents, and training completion. Each operator chooses its own set.

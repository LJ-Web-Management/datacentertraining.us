## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Data centers use layered security: a site perimeter, the building entrance, the data hall, and then cages, cabinets and racks. Every layer only works if people follow the rules at the door. Tailgating through a secured entry, an unescorted vendor or a borrowed badge can bypass every layer at once.

Customers and auditors expect this discipline. PCI DSS requires controls on physical access to cardholder data systems, and SOC 2 and ISO/IEC 27001 audits review physical access records. This foundational course gives floor staff the basics: how badge and access control systems work, visitor and vendor escort procedures, how to spot and close common security gaps, and how to report incidents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is tailgating in a data center?

Tailgating, also called piggybacking, is when someone follows an authorized person through a secured door without using their own credentials. Mantraps, anti-passback rules and trained staff who challenge unknown people are the main defenses.

### Why do vendors need an escort?

Vendors often need access to sensitive areas but are not regular badge holders. Escort rules make sure someone accountable is with them, keeps them to the approved area and records the visit. Escort logs are commonly reviewed during audits.

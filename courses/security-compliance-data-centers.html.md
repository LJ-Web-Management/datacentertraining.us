Comprehensive Program

Build a facility security program that satisfies SOC 2, ISO 27001, and physical audit requirements.

16 hrs · self-paced online Security & Compliance SOC 2 / ISO 27001

Data center security spans two very different disciplines — physical security architecture and facility cybersecurity — and most compliance frameworks expect both to work together. This course covers physical security design (access control, surveillance, perimeter), facility cybersecurity fundamentals, and the regulatory frameworks (SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, PCI-DSS) that shape audit requirements.

You'll build a security roadmap and facility-specific security policy using the audit framework development, compliance policy templates, and vendor evaluation strategies covered throughout the course — not generic advice, but the actual deliverables an auditor expects to see.

The course also covers incident response planning and business continuity for security events, so your program covers detection and response, not just prevention.

## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Data center customers increasingly require proof of security, not just promises. SOC 2 reports, ISO/IEC 27001 certification, PCI DSS assessments and HIPAA obligations all include physical safeguards, and many also reach into facility systems such as building controls, badge systems and camera networks, which can be entry points for cyberattacks.

Passing an audit takes more than good equipment. Controls must be designed, operated consistently and documented, and incident response plans must exist and be tested. This program covers physical security architecture, access control and surveillance, cybersecurity for facility systems, the main regulatory frameworks, incident response planning and compliance audit procedures.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between SOC 2 and ISO 27001?

SOC 2 is an attestation report issued by an independent CPA firm against the AICPA Trust Services Criteria. ISO/IEC 27001 is an international standard for information security management systems, with certification by an accredited body. Both include physical security controls, and many data centers maintain both.

### Why do facility systems need cybersecurity?

Building management, power monitoring, badge and camera systems are often connected to networks. If they are poorly secured, attackers can use them to disrupt operations or reach other systems. The course has a dedicated module on cybersecurity for facility systems.

### Does this course prepare my facility for an audit?

It covers the frameworks, controls and audit procedures that auditors expect and how to prepare evidence. The audit itself is performed by an independent assessor or certification body.

### Is there a data center security certification?

There is no single required one. For people, common choices are ASIS International's Physical Security Professional (PSP) and Certified Protection Professional (CPP). For the facility, SOC 2 reports and ISO/IEC 27001 certification are audits of the organization, not credentials for staff. This course awards a certificate of completion and prepares staff for the controls those audits examine.

### What does a data center security audit check?

Auditors check that physical and logical controls exist and work as written: perimeter and building access, visitor and contractor escorts, badge and mantrap logs, CCTV coverage and retention, equipment chain of custody, periodic access reviews and incident records. Frameworks such as SOC 2, ISO/IEC 27001 and PCI DSS (Requirement 9 covers physical access) set the specific criteria.

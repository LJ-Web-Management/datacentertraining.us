## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Many data halls use clean agent fire suppression instead of, or alongside, sprinklers. Clean agents extinguish a fire without leaving water or residue on equipment. Some agents, such as HFC-227ea (known by trade names such as FM-200), work mainly by absorbing heat, while inert gas systems such as IG-541 work by lowering oxygen in the room. Either way, a discharge is loud and sudden, and people need to leave the room when the pre-discharge alarm sounds.

Clean agent systems are designed and maintained to NFPA 2001. Most have a time delay between the alarm and discharge, and many include an abort switch. Staff need to recognize the alarms, know evacuation routes and understand what not to do after a discharge, such as re-entering before the room is ventilated. This course covers clean agent basics, alarm recognition, evacuation and post-discharge safety.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are clean agent fire suppression systems safe for people?

Systems designed to NFPA 2001 use agent concentrations that are considered safe for short exposure, but people should still evacuate when the alarm sounds. A discharge can cause low visibility, noise, cold temperatures and, with inert gas systems, reduced oxygen. Fire also produces toxic combustion products.

### What does the abort switch do?

On many systems, the abort switch delays or stops the countdown to discharge while it is held, for example if the alarm was caused by a false detection. Its exact behavior depends on the system design, so staff must learn their facility's procedure.

### When is it safe to re-enter after a discharge?

Only after the fire department or the facility's designated authority says so, and after the room has been ventilated. The course's post-discharge module covers the checks and steps involved.

### What fire detection and suppression requirements should data center compliance teams evaluate?

Start with the codes your jurisdiction has adopted, then check each system against its standard: NFPA 75 for protection of IT equipment, NFPA 72 for fire alarm and detection, NFPA 2001 for clean agent systems, NFPA 13 for sprinklers and NFPA 25 for sprinkler inspection, testing and maintenance. Confirm current inspection and test records, enclosure integrity for clean agent rooms, abort and release procedures, and staff training records.

### How should a data center restore compliance after a suppression discharge or a failed fire inspection?

Treat it as a system impairment. Notify the monitoring company and the authority having jurisdiction as your fire code requires, provide a fire watch or other interim protection while the system is out of service, have a qualified contractor recharge or replace agent containers and reset the system, and repair damaged fire barriers or penetrations. Then retest, re-check enclosure integrity where barriers changed, and document every corrective action.

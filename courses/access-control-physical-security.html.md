## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Most physical security failures in a data center are not break-ins. They are a badge that still works after a contractor's job ended, a door propped open during a delivery, a camera that stopped recording weeks ago, or a tailgater nobody challenged. Those gaps are operational, and the people who close them are the security officers and facilities staff who run the systems every shift.

Auditors look for the same thing. SOC 2, ISO/IEC 27001 and PCI DSS assessments all ask for evidence that physical controls actually operate: access reviews, visitor logs, footage retention and incident records. When an incident does happen, how evidence and removed media are handled decides whether the facility can show what happened. This course focuses on running access control and surveillance day to day, recognizing threats early, and documenting incidents with a clean chain of custody.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is chain of custody and why does it matter in a data center?

Chain of custody is the documented record of who held an item, such as a failed drive, a badge or recorded footage, from the moment it was collected until it was disposed of or handed over. Without it, a facility cannot prove that customer data or evidence was not tampered with, which matters for audits, customer contracts and any investigation.

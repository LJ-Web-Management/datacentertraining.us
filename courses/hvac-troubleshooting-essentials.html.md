## Why This Matters in a Data Center

When a cooling unit fails in a data hall, temperatures can rise quickly, especially in high-density rows. The technician on site needs to find the cause fast: a failed compressor, a refrigerant problem, a stuck valve, a bad sensor or a controls issue. Guessing and swapping parts wastes time the room does not have.

Effective troubleshooting starts with the refrigeration cycle and the numbers that describe it, such as pressures, superheat and subcooling, plus enough psychrometrics to understand dew point and humidity. It also means knowing the difference between direct-expansion CRAC units and chilled-water CRAH units. This course covers refrigeration cycle fundamentals, CRAC/CRAH diagnostics, common failures and maintenance practices that prevent repeat problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are superheat and subcooling?

Superheat is how much the refrigerant vapor temperature is above its boiling point at the evaporator outlet. Subcooling is how much the liquid is below its condensing temperature at the condenser outlet. Together they help diagnose charge problems, restrictions and valve issues.

### Why does humidity matter in a data center?

High humidity can cause condensation and corrosion, while very low humidity increases static discharge risk. ASHRAE's thermal guidelines give recommended ranges based on dew point and relative humidity, and cooling units must be tuned to stay within them.

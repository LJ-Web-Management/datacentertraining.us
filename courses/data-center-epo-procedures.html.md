## Why This Matters in a Data Center

An emergency power off (EPO) system can drop power to an entire data hall in an instant. It exists to protect people and first responders during a fire or electrical emergency. Pressed by mistake, it causes a full outage. Accidental EPO activations, from someone mistaking the button for a door release or bumping an unguarded switch, are a well-known cause of downtime.

NFPA 70 (the National Electrical Code) Article 645 addresses disconnecting means for information technology equipment rooms, and many facilities also coordinate the EPO with fire alarm and suppression systems. Staff with floor access need to know when activation is justified, what the EPO shuts down, who must be notified and how to restore power safely afterward. This course covers EPO system design, activation criteria, fire and life safety coordination, and reset procedures.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does an EPO button do in a data center?

It disconnects power to IT equipment and, depending on the design, to cooling and other systems in the protected room. Its purpose is to remove power quickly during an emergency so people and firefighters are not exposed to energized equipment.

### How can accidental EPO activation be prevented?

Common measures include protective covers, clear labeling, placing buttons away from door releases, two-step activation designs and training everyone with floor access. The course's activation criteria module covers when the button should and should not be used.

### Why is resetting an EPO a separate procedure?

After an EPO event, power must be restored in a controlled sequence after the cause is understood. Bringing systems back all at once can cause inrush problems, restart equipment into a hazard or damage IT loads. A written reset procedure prevents a second incident.

### What does EPO mean in electrical terms?

EPO stands for emergency power off: a control, usually a red button near the exits of an IT room, that disconnects power to the room's electronic equipment in one action. Electrical codes call it a remote disconnecting means. In rooms designed under NEC Article 645, the same action also shuts down the room's dedicated HVAC system.

### Is an EPO required in a data center?

Not always. The National Electrical Code requires a remote disconnect when an IT equipment room is designed under Article 645, which permits special wiring methods in exchange. Many operators design rooms outside Article 645 to avoid accidental shutdowns, while others keep an EPO by choice or because the local authority having jurisdiction requires one. Check your design basis and local code.

## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Power problems are the leading cause of significant data center outages in industry surveys, which is why the electrical system gets more redundancy than almost anything else in the building. A single design or operating mistake anywhere from the utility service to the rack can drop critical load.

This program follows the full power chain. It starts with utility interconnection and medium-voltage switchgear, moves through transformers, UPS systems and PDUs, and covers redundancy models such as N+1, 2N and distributed redundant designs. It also covers monitoring, maintenance and safety, including NFPA 70E. Electricians, power engineers and technicians come away understanding how each part affects availability and how to maintain it safely.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does a typical data center power chain look like?

Utility power enters through medium-voltage switchgear and is stepped down by transformers. It passes through switchboards to UPS systems, which provide ride-through during outages, then to PDUs and remote power panels, and finally to rack PDUs and servers. Generators back up the utility.

### What is distributed redundancy?

Distributed redundant designs, sometimes called 3-to-make-2 or 4-to-make-3, spread load across several UPS systems so that the failure of one can be absorbed by the others. They can offer high availability with less idle equipment than a full 2N design.

### Who should take this program?

Electricians, power engineers, facilities technicians and project engineers who work on or specify critical power systems, plus operations staff who want a full picture of the electrical infrastructure they rely on.

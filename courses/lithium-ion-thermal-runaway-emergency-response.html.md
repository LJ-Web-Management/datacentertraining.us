## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Lithium-ion batteries are replacing lead-acid in many UPS systems because they are smaller, lighter and last longer. They also bring a different emergency: thermal runaway, where a cell overheats and releases flammable and toxic gases, and heat can spread from cell to cell. A lithium-ion battery fire can reignite after it appears to be out.

Codes and test methods have developed quickly. NFPA 855 covers the installation of stationary energy storage systems, and UL 9540A is the test method used to evaluate how thermal runaway spreads. Facility staff need to recognize early warning signs such as off-gas alarms and abnormal temperatures, know the facility's containment steps and coordinate with the fire department. This course covers thermal runaway fundamentals, early warning signs, containment and emergency response coordination.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is thermal runaway?

Thermal runaway is a chain reaction inside a battery cell where rising temperature causes reactions that release more heat. The cell can vent flammable and toxic gases and may ignite. In a battery module, heat can spread to neighboring cells.

### How is a lithium-ion incident different from a lead-acid battery incident?

Lead-acid batteries mainly pose hydrogen gas, electrolyte and electrical hazards. Lithium-ion batteries can go into self-sustaining thermal runaway, release large amounts of flammable gas quickly and reignite. Early detection and response planning are different as a result.

### What should staff do if a lithium-ion battery alarm activates?

Follow the facility's emergency procedure: evacuate the area if required, notify the fire department and site management, and avoid entering a room that may contain flammable gas. The course's response coordination module covers how to prepare these procedures.

## Why This Matters in a Data Center

A roof leak above an office damages ceiling tiles. A roof leak above a data hall can reach energized equipment, trigger an emergency shutdown or force a customer migration. Data center roofs also carry more than weather: condensers, dry coolers, exhaust fans, antennas and the foot traffic of technicians who service them.

Most roof failures give warning signs first: ponding water, blocked drains, open seams, damaged flashing around penetrations or punctures near rooftop equipment. Common roof types such as TPO, EPDM, modified bitumen and built-up roofs each have typical weak points. Many facilities inspect at least twice a year and after major storms. This course covers roofing system types, inspection scheduling, early failure signs and risk-based prioritization of repairs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should a data center roof be inspected?

A common practice is at least twice a year, often in spring and fall, plus after severe weather. Roofs with a lot of rooftop equipment or a history of leaks may need more frequent checks.

### What are the most common causes of roof leaks?

Failed flashing around penetrations and curbs, open seams, blocked drains that cause ponding, and punctures from foot traffic or dropped tools near rooftop equipment. Many of these can be found early with regular inspections.

### How should roof repairs be prioritized?

By risk: what is directly above critical equipment, how likely the defect is to leak and how bad the consequence would be. The course's risk-based prioritization module covers how to rank repairs so the most important work is done first.

## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Cooling equipment combines several hazards in one place: refrigerant under high pressure, rotating fans and compressors, electrical panels and hot surfaces. A refrigerant release in an enclosed mechanical room can displace oxygen, and contact with liquid refrigerant can cause frostbite. Cooling units also have to be locked out properly, because a unit that restarts automatically can injure someone working inside it.

Refrigerant work is also regulated. Under Section 608 of the Clean Air Act, EPA requires technicians who open refrigerant circuits to be certified and sets rules for leak repair and recovery. The industry is moving to lower-GWP refrigerants under the AIM Act HFC phasedown, and some replacements, such as A2L refrigerants, are mildly flammable. This course covers refrigerant handling basics, CRAC/CRAH hazards, lockout/tagout for cooling systems and how to respond to leaks and exposures.

## Safety Scope & Limits

### Applicable standards and regulations

- EPA Clean Air Act Section 608 refrigerant management rules (40 CFR Part 82, Subpart F)
- OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 (control of hazardous energy) and general duty to protect workers from refrigerant exposure hazards

### What this course does

- Covers refrigerant and cooling system hazards in CRAC/CRAH environments and the safe practices around them.

### What it does not do

- Does not provide EPA Section 608 technician certification. Federal rules require that certification to maintain, service, repair, or dispose of equipment that could release refrigerant.
- Does not qualify you to open or service refrigerant circuits.

An online course alone does not make anyone qualified for hazardous work. Under OSHA rules, the employer decides who is qualified, based on training plus demonstrated skill on the specific equipment and tasks at your site. Referencing a standard does not mean the organization that publishes it has reviewed or endorsed this course.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does this course provide EPA Section 608 certification?

No. EPA 608 certification requires passing an EPA-approved exam. This course covers safe handling practices and hazard recognition for data center cooling systems, which supports technicians who hold or are working toward that certification.

### What are A2L refrigerants?

A2L is the ASHRAE Standard 34 classification for refrigerants with lower toxicity and lower flammability. Several low-GWP replacements for older HFCs are A2L, so technicians need to understand their handling and ventilation requirements as new equipment arrives.

### Why does lockout/tagout matter for cooling units?

CRAC and CRAH units can restart on their own through controls or redundancy logic. Without proper lockout, fans, compressors or electrical parts can energize while someone is working on them. The course has a dedicated module on LOTO for cooling systems.

## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Data centers have more environmental obligations than many people expect. Standby diesel generators often need air permits and are subject to EPA engine rules that limit non-emergency run hours. Large fuel storage can require a Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure (SPCC) plan. Batteries, refrigerants, stormwater and hazardous waste can all bring their own reporting and record-keeping requirements.

Missed reports and expired permits can lead to fines and, in some cases, limits on running generators when you need them. Requirements also vary by state and local agency, so each facility needs its own register of obligations. This course gives facility managers an overview of the main environmental regulations, permit and reporting requirements, compliance audit procedures and documentation practices, with checklist templates you can adapt to your permits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do data center generators need environmental permits?

Often, yes. Depending on engine size, number of engines and location, standby generators may need a state or local air permit and must meet federal engine standards. Emergency engines generally have limits on hours for maintenance and testing. Your permit and local agency set the specific terms.

### What is an SPCC plan?

A Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure plan is required by EPA for facilities that store more than set thresholds of oil, including diesel fuel, above ground and could reasonably discharge to navigable waters. It describes how spills are prevented, contained and cleaned up.

### Does this course replace legal or permit advice?

No. It explains common regulations and how to build a compliance program. Your permits, state regulators and environmental counsel remain the authority on your facility's specific obligations.

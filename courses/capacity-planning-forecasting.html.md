## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Data centers rarely run out of floor space first. They run out of power, cooling or both, often while rack positions are still empty. Rising rack densities for AI and high-performance workloads have made this faster: a row designed for 5–10 kW per rack cannot simply absorb racks that draw several times that.

Capacity problems are expensive when discovered late. Adding utility service, switchgear or chillers can take many months or years, so planners need forecasts well before they are out of headroom. Stranded capacity, where power is available but cooling is not (or the reverse), wastes investment. This course covers workload characterization, power and cooling demand forecasting, constraint analysis and expansion planning, so you can see limits coming and make the case for investment on time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is stranded capacity?

Stranded capacity is power, cooling or space that exists but cannot be used because another resource has run out. For example, a room might have spare UPS capacity but no cooling left in that zone. Constraint analysis helps find and recover it.

### How far ahead should a data center forecast capacity?

Far enough to cover the lead time of the largest upgrade you might need. Utility upgrades, generators and major cooling plant changes can take a long time to design, procure and install, so many teams plan on multi-year horizons and update the forecast regularly.

### Who is this course for?

Facility and IT planners, capacity managers and engineering leads responsible for growth forecasts, expansion budgets or deciding where new customer or workload deployments can go.

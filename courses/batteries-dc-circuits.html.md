## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Every UPS in a data center ends in a battery string, and every battery string is a DC circuit. When a technician misreads a string's voltage, misses a high-resistance connection or does not understand how temperature affects capacity, the result is a UPS that looks healthy on its display but cannot carry the load when utility power drops.

DC behaves differently from the AC most electricians learn first. Strings are built from cells in series and parallel, stored energy is present even when the UPS is off, and internal resistance rises slowly as a battery ages. Heat is a major factor: a common rule of thumb for VRLA batteries is that service life roughly halves for every 8°C (about 15°F) above 25°C (77°F). This course builds that DC foundation so readings, alarms and test results make sense before you work on UPS and battery systems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do data center technicians need to learn DC theory?

UPS batteries, many telecom systems and some rack power designs run on DC. Understanding series and parallel strings, voltage drop and internal resistance lets you interpret battery test results, spot a failing cell and work safely around stored energy that does not go away when AC power is switched off.

### Does this course cover lithium-ion as well as lead-acid batteries?

The Battery Chemistry Basics module explains why different battery chemistries behave the way they do under load and during charging. For the specific hazards and emergency response for lithium-ion installations, see [Lithium-Ion Battery Thermal Runaway and Emergency Response Training](https://datacentertraining.us/courses/lithium-ion-thermal-runaway-emergency-response.html).

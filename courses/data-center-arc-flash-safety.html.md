## Why This Matters in a Data Center

An arc flash is an explosive release of energy from an electrical fault through the air. It can produce extreme heat, pressure and molten metal in a fraction of a second. Data centers concentrate large amounts of power in switchgear, UPS systems and distribution equipment, and the fault current available in those rooms can be higher than in a typical commercial building.

NFPA 70E requires employers to assess arc flash risk, label equipment, set boundaries and select PPE based on the hazard. The standard also requires the arc flash risk assessment to be reviewed at intervals of no more than five years, or whenever the system changes significantly. This course explains arc flash fundamentals, how to read boundaries and labels, how PPE is selected, safe work practices and the risk factors specific to data center equipment.

## Safety Scope & Limits

### Applicable standards and regulations

- NFPA 70E, *Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace* (arc flash risk assessment, boundaries, labeling, PPE)
- OSHA 29 CFR 1910.331–1910.335 (electrical safety-related work practices)

### What this course does

- Explains arc flash hazards, boundaries, labels, and PPE selection so you can recognize risk and follow your site's procedures.
- Supports the classroom portion of your employer's electrical safety training.

### What it does not do

- Does not make you a *qualified person* for work on or near exposed energized parts. That designation comes from your employer after task- and equipment-specific training and demonstrated skill.
- Does not authorize you to perform lockout/tagout, energized work, or switching. Follow your site's electrical safety program and written procedures.
- Does not replace hands-on PPE fitting, equipment-specific instruction, or field evaluation.

An online course alone does not make anyone qualified for hazardous work. Under OSHA rules, the employer decides who is qualified, based on training plus demonstrated skill on the specific equipment and tasks at your site. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the arc flash boundary?

Under NFPA 70E, the arc flash boundary is the distance from exposed energized parts at which a person without arc-rated PPE could receive a second-degree burn, defined as an incident energy of 1.2 cal/cm². Anyone inside that boundary must wear PPE rated for the hazard.

### What information is on an arc flash label?

An arc flash label typically shows the nominal voltage, the arc flash boundary, and either the incident energy at a working distance or the PPE category, along with other details required by NFPA 70E. Workers use it to decide what PPE and precautions the task needs.

### Does this course make me a qualified electrical worker?

No single course does that. Under OSHA and NFPA 70E, the employer decides who is qualified based on training and demonstrated skills for specific equipment and tasks. This course supports that training for work near energized data center equipment.

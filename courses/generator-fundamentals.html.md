## Why This Matters in a Data Center

A standby generator is only useful if it starts, reaches stable voltage and frequency, and accepts load within seconds of an outage. Understanding how the engine, alternator, fuel system and controls work together is what lets a technician read an alarm correctly or spot a problem during a weekly inspection.

Common failure points are well known: weak starting batteries, fuel that has degraded or picked up water and microbial growth, coolant heater failures and control faults. Diesel engines that run too long at light load can also suffer wet stacking, a buildup of unburned fuel in the exhaust. This course covers engine and alternator basics, fuel systems, control panels and alarms, and how that theory connects to load-bank testing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is wet stacking?

Wet stacking is the buildup of unburned fuel and carbon in a diesel engine's exhaust system, usually caused by running the engine at light load for long periods. It reduces performance and can be prevented by exercising the generator at adequate load or running periodic load-bank tests.

### Why does diesel fuel need to be tested?

Stored diesel degrades over time, can absorb water and can support microbial growth that clogs filters. Periodic fuel sampling and treatment help make sure the generator can run for its full design time during an outage.

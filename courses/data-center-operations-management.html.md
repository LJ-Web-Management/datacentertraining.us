## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Industry outage surveys consistently find that a large share of data center incidents involve human error or process failures rather than equipment design. A skipped step in a maintenance procedure, an unclear shift handover or an untested emergency plan can take down a facility that was designed to stay up.

Operations management turns a facility into a reliable service. It includes written procedures (SOPs, MOPs and EOPs), change control, preventive maintenance, DCIM and asset tracking, incident management and the service level metrics customers see. It also includes energy management, because operating decisions drive a large part of the power bill. This program covers the full operations lifecycle for facility managers and operations directors responsible for daily uptime.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are SOPs, MOPs and EOPs?

Standard operating procedures (SOPs) cover routine tasks. Methods of procedure (MOPs) are step-by-step plans for specific maintenance or change work, including backout steps. Emergency operating procedures (EOPs) tell staff what to do during failures. Together they make operations repeatable and reduce human error.

### How are data center SLAs usually measured?

Common metrics include availability of power and cooling, environmental conditions at the rack, response and resolution times for incidents and completion of scheduled maintenance. The SLA Metrics & Performance Tracking module covers how to define and report them.

### Who should take this program?

Facility managers, operations directors, shift leads and engineers moving into operations leadership. It is also useful for colocation and enterprise teams formalizing their operations program.

### What does data center operations management cover?

It covers how a facility is run day to day: staffing and shift handovers, maintenance planning, change management, incident response, monitoring through DCIM and BMS, SLA reporting and energy management. This program works through each area as a full curriculum for supervisors and managers.

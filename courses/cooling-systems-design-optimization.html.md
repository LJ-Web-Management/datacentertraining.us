# Cooling Systems Design & Optimization

Comprehensive Program

Cut cooling costs and improve reliability with modern thermal management strategy.

8 hrs · self-paced online Cooling & Efficiency PUE / ASHRAE Thermal Guidelines

Cooling is typically the second-largest line item in a data center's power bill, and the technology has moved fast — liquid cooling, containment, and free cooling have all become mainstream options in the last decade. This course covers CRAC/CRAH fundamentals, liquid cooling technologies (direct-to-chip, immersion), containment strategies, free cooling, and the thermal analysis behind PUE optimization.

You'll work through cost-benefit analysis of different cooling technologies and energy modeling concepts, using case studies of facilities that achieved 30–50% energy improvements through better cooling strategy — not just newer equipment.

The goal is a cooling optimization strategy you can actually implement: knowing which technology fits your facility's density and climate, and how to build the business case for the investment.

## Why This Matters in a Data Center

Cooling usually uses more energy than anything in a data center except the IT load itself, and it is changing fast. Higher rack densities from AI and HPC workloads are pushing air cooling to its limits, and direct-to-chip liquid cooling, rear-door heat exchangers and containment are now common. Decisions made today have to fit both current racks and the next generation of hardware.

Good cooling design is about matching the system to the load: getting cold air to server inlets without mixing, running at the warmer inlet temperatures allowed by the ASHRAE thermal guidelines (a recommended range of 18–27°C, or 64.4–80.6°F, for most equipment) and using economizers when the climate allows. This program covers CRAC/CRAH fundamentals, liquid cooling, containment and airflow management, free cooling, thermal modeling and the energy economics that connect cooling choices to PUE and operating costs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a CRAC and a CRAH?

A CRAC (computer room air conditioner) has its own compressor and refrigeration circuit. A CRAH (computer room air handler) uses chilled water from a central plant and a fan to move air across a coil. The choice affects efficiency, redundancy and how the rest of the plant is designed.

### Is liquid cooling replacing air cooling?

Not entirely. Liquid cooling is growing quickly for high-density racks, especially for AI hardware, but most facilities will run a mix of air and liquid cooling for years. The course covers both and how to decide which fits a given load.

### How does cooling design affect PUE?

PUE is total facility energy divided by IT energy, and cooling is usually the largest non-IT load. Containment, higher supply temperatures and economizer hours can all lower cooling energy and PUE. The final module ties these choices to energy costs.

Refrigeration Safety Training: Refrigerant Handling and Safety Programs

Chillers and refrigeration systems hold large charges of refrigerant under pressure, and the refrigerants themselves are changing as HFCs are phased down. Here is what refrigeration safety training covers, from EPA Section 608 certification to industrial ammonia process safety, and how to build a refrigeration safety program for a data center or industrial facility.

Short answer: Refrigeration safety training teaches workers to handle refrigerants and work on refrigeration equipment without injury or illegal release. It covers refrigerant hazards (asphyxiation, frostbite, toxicity, flammability for A2L and A3 refrigerants), pressure and lockout, leak detection and machinery room safety under ASHRAE 15, and EPA Section 608 rules for recovery and certification. Industrial ammonia systems add OSHA Process Safety Management and EPA Risk Management Program requirements and IIAR standards.

Key takeaways

  • Anyone who opens a refrigerant circuit on stationary equipment must hold the matching EPA Section 608 certification.
  • ASHRAE 15 sets safety rules for refrigeration systems and machinery rooms; ASHRAE 34 classifies refrigerants by toxicity and flammability.
  • Mildly flammable A2L refrigerants are replacing many HFCs under the AIM Act, so technicians need new handling and tool training.
  • Ammonia systems above 10,000 pounds fall under OSHA Process Safety Management and usually EPA's Risk Management Program.
  • A refrigeration safety program combines training, written procedures, leak detection, emergency response, and mechanical integrity.

Refrigerant handling certification: EPA Section 608

Under Section 608 of the Clean Air Act, technicians who maintain, service, repair, or dispose of equipment that could release refrigerant must be certified. There are four types: Type I for small appliances, Type II for high-pressure equipment, Type III for low-pressure equipment such as many centrifugal chillers, and Universal for all three. The rules also cover recovery, leak repair for larger systems, and recordkeeping. Data center chillers can be high- or low-pressure, so check which certification your equipment requires.

A2L refrigerants and the HFC phasedown

The AIM Act directs EPA to phase down HFC production and consumption, and EPA's technology transitions rules restrict high global warming potential refrigerants in new equipment by sector. Many new systems use lower-GWP refrigerants classified A2L (lower toxicity, mildly flammable) under ASHRAE 34, such as R-32 and R-454B, and some chillers use HFOs. A2L work needs updated training on ignition source control, compatible recovery equipment and detectors, cylinder identification, and the updated safety requirements in ASHRAE 15 and the mechanical codes.

Refrigeration safety standards

  • ANSI/ASHRAE 15: safety standard for refrigeration systems, including machinery room ventilation, detection, and relief.
  • ANSI/ASHRAE 34: designation and safety classification of refrigerants.
  • UL 60335-2-40 and the mechanical codes: equipment safety and installation requirements, including for A2L systems.
  • OSHA: lockout/tagout (1910.147), hazard communication, respiratory protection, and confined space rules all apply to refrigeration work.

Industrial refrigeration safety programs and ammonia

Industrial refrigeration, common in food processing, cold storage, and some district cooling plants, often uses anhydrous ammonia. Facilities with more than 10,000 pounds of ammonia in a process fall under OSHA's Process Safety Management standard (29 CFR 1910.119) and usually EPA's Risk Management Program. In California, Cal/OSHA's PSM rules and the CalARP program apply. PSM requires process hazard analysis, written operating procedures, training with documented refreshers, mechanical integrity, management of change, incident investigation, and emergency planning. The International Institute of All-Natural Refrigeration (IIAR) publishes the standards most ammonia programs follow, and the Refrigerating Engineers and Technicians Association (RETA) offers operator certifications such as CARO and CIRO.

How to build a refrigeration safety program

  1. Inventory systems and refrigerants

    List every system, refrigerant type, safety class, and charge size. This decides which regulations apply.

  2. Write procedures

    Standard operating procedures for startup, shutdown, charging, recovery, and leak response, plus lockout/tagout procedures for each system.

  3. Train and certify

    EPA 608 certification for anyone opening circuits, hazard awareness for everyone who enters machinery rooms, and PSM training where it applies.

  4. Detect and ventilate

    Maintain refrigerant detectors and machinery room ventilation and test them on schedule.

  5. Plan for emergencies

    An emergency action plan for leaks, coordinated with the local fire department.

  6. Keep records

    Leak inspections, repairs, refrigerant added and recovered, and training records.

Frequently asked questions

What is refrigeration safety training?

Refrigeration safety training teaches workers to handle refrigerants and work on refrigeration equipment safely and legally. It covers refrigerant hazards, pressure and lockout, leak detection, machinery room safety, A2L flammable refrigerants, EPA Section 608 rules, and emergency response.

Do I need EPA 608 certification to handle refrigerant?

Yes, if you maintain, service, repair, or dispose of stationary equipment in a way that could release refrigerant. The certification type (I, II, III, or Universal) must match the equipment.

What is an industrial refrigeration safety program?

It is the set of written procedures, training, inspections, mechanical integrity work, and emergency planning that keeps an industrial refrigeration system, often ammonia, safe. Systems with more than 10,000 pounds of ammonia must meet OSHA Process Safety Management requirements.

What are A2L refrigerants?

A2L refrigerants are classified by ASHRAE 34 as lower toxicity and mildly flammable. Refrigerants such as R-32 and R-454B are replacing higher-GWP HFCs in many new systems and require updated handling training and equipment.

Do data centers use ammonia refrigeration?

Rarely in the white space, but some large chiller plants and district cooling systems use ammonia. Most data center chillers use HFC, HFO, or blended refrigerants, which still require EPA 608 certified technicians.

Preventive Maintenance Training: Courses for Technicians and Planners

Preventive maintenance is where reliability is won or lost, and it depends on two skills: planning the right work and doing it right. Here is what preventive maintenance training covers for technicians, maintenance planners, and managers, how predictive maintenance fits in, and a course path that applies to data centers and any facility with critical equipment.

Short answer: Preventive maintenance training teaches how to keep equipment reliable through scheduled inspection, testing, and servicing. Technician training covers the tasks themselves for mechanical, electrical, and building systems, done safely and recorded properly. Maintenance planner training covers building job plans, setting frequencies, scheduling by calendar or operating hours in a CMMS, coordinating outages, and managing backlog. Manager training covers program design and KPIs.

Key takeaways

  • Technicians, planners, and managers each need different preventive maintenance training.
  • Planning decides what work is done and how; scheduling decides when. Both are learnable skills.
  • Predictive maintenance (thermography, vibration, oil analysis) is the next step after a solid PM program.
  • In critical facilities, PM training must include safe isolation, change control, and redundancy checks.
  • Online courses cover the methods; site training covers your equipment and your CMMS.

Maintenance planner training

A maintenance planner prepares work so that technicians can do it efficiently and safely. Planner training typically covers:

  • Building an asset hierarchy and ranking assets by criticality.
  • Writing job plans with steps, tools, parts, permits, and estimated hours.
  • Setting PM frequencies from manufacturer guidance, standards, and failure history.
  • Choosing calendar, meter (operating hours), or condition triggers.
  • Loading and maintaining the CMMS so work orders generate correctly.
  • Building a weekly schedule against available labor and coordinating equipment outages.
  • Measuring PM compliance, backlog, and planned-to-unplanned ratio.

Preventive maintenance training for technicians

Technicians need to know the equipment, the task, and the safety controls. Mechanical systems training covers pumps, fans, motors, bearings, belts, valves, chillers, and air handlers. Electrical PM requires qualified-person training under NFPA 70E. Building systems cover roofs, envelopes, doors, and life safety equipment. Good PM training also teaches technicians to record useful data: a reading in the CMMS is worth more than "checked OK".

Predictive maintenance training

Predictive maintenance uses measurements to find developing problems before they cause failures. The common techniques are infrared thermography for electrical connections, vibration analysis for rotating equipment, oil analysis for engines and compressors, ultrasonic testing for leaks and electrical discharge, and battery impedance trending. Each has its own certification paths for analysts. A technician who understands what the readings mean can spot a trend long before an alarm.

Preventive maintenance in critical facilities

In a data center, hospital, or other critical facility, maintenance work is itself a risk. Training must include confirming redundancy before taking equipment out of service, working from approved methods of procedure, lockout/tagout, and returning equipment to normal with a verification step. Many outages happen during or right after maintenance, so these steps are part of the PM, not extras.

Frequently asked questions

What is preventive maintenance training?

Preventive maintenance training teaches how to keep equipment reliable through scheduled inspection, testing, and servicing. It covers the maintenance tasks themselves, safe work practices, recording results, and, for planners, how to build job plans and schedules in a CMMS.

What does a maintenance planner do?

A maintenance planner prepares maintenance work before it is scheduled: defining the steps, tools, parts, permits, and labor needed, setting task frequencies, and keeping the CMMS accurate so technicians can work efficiently and safely.

What is the difference between preventive and predictive maintenance?

Preventive maintenance is done on a fixed schedule, by calendar or operating hours. Predictive maintenance is triggered by measured condition, such as a rising temperature in a thermal scan or increasing vibration on a bearing.

Is there a preventive maintenance certification?

There are professional certifications in maintenance and reliability, such as the Certified Maintenance and Reliability Professional (CMRP) from SMRP, and analyst certifications for predictive techniques like vibration and thermography. Course certificates document training in PM methods.

Can I take preventive maintenance training online?

Yes. Planning methods, scheduling, frequencies, and equipment fundamentals are well suited to online courses. Hands-on skills on specific equipment come from site training and supervised work.

# Commissioning and Startup Training for Data Centers

Training · Commissioning

Every new data center is proven through commissioning: equipment is started up, tested alone, and then tested together until the facility survives a simulated utility failure. The people who plan and witness that testing need specific skills. Here is what commissioning training covers, the certifications in the field, and how technicians and engineers get into commissioning work.

**Short answer:** Commissioning training teaches the process of verifying that a facility's systems are installed, started up, and perform as designed. For data centers it covers the five commissioning levels (factory testing, site inspection, startup, functional testing, and integrated systems testing), writing and running test scripts, startup of UPS, generators, switchgear, and cooling, issue tracking, and turnover. Certifications such as ACG's CxA, BCxA's CCP, and ASHRAE's CPMP recognize experienced commissioning professionals.

Key takeaways

- Commissioning (Cx) verifies that systems are installed and work as designed, individually and together.
- Data center commissioning is organized into five levels, ending with integrated systems testing (IST).
- Startup is manufacturer-led and comes before functional testing; commissioning witnesses and documents it.
- Commissioning credentials come from ACG, BCxA, ASHRAE, AEE, and NEBB, and most require field experience.
- Commissioning is a common bridge from technician to engineer roles, and from construction to operations.

## What commissioning and startup training covers

- **The commissioning process:** owner's project requirements, basis of design, the commissioning plan, and how ASHRAE Guideline 0 and Standard 202 describe the process.
- **Roles:** owner, designer, contractor, manufacturer, and commissioning authority (CxA), and who signs off on what.
- **Test scripts:** writing pre-functional checklists and functional performance tests with clear acceptance criteria.
- **Startup:** manufacturer startup procedures for UPS, generators, switchgear, chillers, and CRAHs, and what commissioning witnesses.
- **Integrated systems testing:** simulating utility failure, generator and UPS transfers, cooling failover, and fire alarm interactions under load banks.
- **Safety:** testing energized systems under NFPA 70E, lockout/tagout, load bank hazards, and coordination with live operations at expansion sites.
- **Issues and turnover:** issue logs, retesting, systems manuals, training records, and handover to operations.

## Data center commissioning levels 1 to 5

**Commissioning levels**

| Level | Name | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Factory witness testing | Major equipment is tested at the factory before shipping |
| 2 | Site inspection and delivery | Equipment is inspected on arrival and after installation |
| 3 | Startup and pre-functional | Manufacturers start up equipment; installation is checked against the design |
| 4 | Functional performance testing | Each system is tested through its modes of operation |
| 5 | Integrated systems testing (IST) | Systems are tested together under load, including utility failure simulations |

For a deeper explanation of each level, including what goes wrong, read [data center commissioning explained](https://datacentertraining.us/learn/data-center-commissioning.html).

## What does UPS startup and commissioning involve for a new build?

UPS startup is performed by the manufacturer's field engineer after the installing contractor finishes wiring. It typically includes inspecting the installation and torque, verifying input and output wiring and phase rotation, checking batteries or energy storage, energizing the system, configuring settings, and confirming alarms and communications. Commissioning then tests the system through its modes: transfer to and from battery, transfer to static and maintenance bypass, module redundancy, load bank testing at stepped loads, and battery discharge testing. In Level 5, the UPS is tested with the generators, transfer switches, and cooling during a simulated utility failure.

## Commissioning certifications

**Common commissioning credentials**

| Credential | Issuer |
| --- | --- |
| Certified Commissioning Authority (CxA) | AABC Commissioning Group (ACG) |
| Certified Commissioning Professional (CCP); Associate Commissioning Professional (ACP) | Building Commissioning Certification Board (BCxA) |
| Commissioning Process Management Professional (CPMP) | ASHRAE |
| Certified Building Commissioning Professional (CBCP) | Association of Energy Engineers (AEE) |
| Building Systems Commissioning certification | NEBB |

Most of these require years of commissioning experience before the exam. Data center specific courses are how technicians and engineers build the knowledge in the meantime.

## Who takes commissioning training?

- Electrical and mechanical technicians moving into commissioning roles with Cx firms or general contractors.
- Operations staff who will inherit a new facility and want to understand how it was tested.
- Project and design engineers who write commissioning requirements.
- Owner's representatives who witness testing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is commissioning training?

Commissioning training teaches the process of verifying that building systems are installed, started up, and perform as designed. For data centers it covers the five commissioning levels, test scripts, equipment startup, integrated systems testing, issue tracking, and turnover to operations.

### What are the levels of data center commissioning?

Level 1 is factory witness testing, Level 2 is site inspection and delivery, Level 3 is startup and pre-functional checks, Level 4 is functional performance testing, and Level 5 is integrated systems testing of everything together under load.

### What certifications are there for commissioning?

Common ones include ACG's Certified Commissioning Authority (CxA), BCxA's Certified Commissioning Professional (CCP), ASHRAE's Commissioning Process Management Professional (CPMP), AEE's Certified Building Commissioning Professional (CBCP), and NEBB's Building Systems Commissioning certification.

### What is the difference between startup and commissioning?

Startup is the manufacturer's process of energizing and configuring a piece of equipment for the first time. Commissioning is the broader process of verifying, documenting, and testing that all systems work as designed, individually and together. Startup is one step within commissioning.

### How do I get into data center commissioning?

Build a foundation in electrical or mechanical systems, take commissioning and electrical safety training, then join a commissioning firm, general contractor, or owner's team to witness and run tests. Many commissioning agents start as electricians, HVAC technicians, or critical facilities technicians.

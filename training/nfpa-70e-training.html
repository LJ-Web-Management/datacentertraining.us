NFPA 70E Training & Certification: Requirements and Online Courses

What NFPA 70E training is, who needs it, what it has to cover, how often you must retrain, and how far an online course can take you. Written for data center electricians, facilities technicians, and the managers who train them.

Short answer: NFPA 70E training teaches workers to recognize and avoid shock and arc flash hazards using the practices in NFPA 70E, the Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace. Anyone exposed to electrical hazards needs training matched to their role, retraining is due at intervals not exceeding three years, and only the employer can designate someone a qualified person.

Key takeaways

  • OSHA does not enforce NFPA 70E directly. OSHA enforces its own electrical rules, which require training, and treats 70E compliance as an acceptable way to control electrical hazards.
  • Qualified persons need deeper training than unqualified persons, but everyone exposed to an electrical hazard needs some training.
  • Retrain at intervals not exceeding three years, and sooner when duties, equipment, or procedures change or a worker is not following safe practices.
  • Online training covers the knowledge. Qualification also takes site-specific instruction, hands-on practice, and demonstrated skill that the employer verifies.

What an online course can and cannot do

An online course builds the knowledge NFPA 70E expects and gives you a record of it. It does not make you a qualified person, authorize you to do lockout/tagout or energized work, or replace hands-on PPE fitting and field evaluation on your site's equipment.

What is NFPA 70E training?

NFPA 70E training is instruction in the safety-related work practices set out in NFPA 70E, the Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, published by the National Fire Protection Association. NFPA revises it on a three-year cycle, and its website lists the 2027 edition as current.

The standard is built around one idea: put equipment in an electrically safe work condition before anyone works on it. That means identifying every source, opening disconnects, applying locks and tags, and testing for absence of voltage. Training explains how to do that, and what to do in the rare cases where energized work is justified:

  • Shock and arc flash risk assessments, and the approach boundaries they produce.
  • Arc flash labels, PPE categories, and how arc-rated clothing is selected.
  • Energized electrical work permits and job briefings.
  • Lockout/tagout and test-before-touch.
  • Selecting and using voltage testers, insulated tools, and other test equipment.
  • Emergency response, including how to safely free a person in contact with an energized part.

In a data center, the equipment changes but the principles stay the same: medium-voltage switchgear, UPS modules and battery strings, PDUs, remote power panels, and busway all carry shock and arc flash hazards.

Is there an NFPA 70E certification?

Not in the sense most job postings mean. OSHA does not certify electrical workers, and there is no government-issued NFPA 70E card. When an employer asks for "NFPA 70E certification," it almost always means a certificate of completion from an NFPA 70E training course, kept in the worker's training file.

That certificate documents training. It does not make anyone a qualified person: only the employer can make that designation, for specific equipment and tasks. Separately, NFPA offers the Certified Electrical Safety Compliance Professional (CESCP), an exam-based credential for people who run electrical safety programs. Check NFPA for its current eligibility rules.

For most data center electricians and technicians, the right goal is a current certificate from 70E-based training, renewed at least every three years, plus documented site-specific qualification.

Is NFPA 70E training required by OSHA?

Not by name. OSHA has said in letters of interpretation that it does not enforce NFPA 70E. It enforces its own electrical standards, and those standards do require training.

Here is how the two fit together:

  • OSHA sets the legal requirement. Its electrical safety-related work practice rules (29 CFR 1910.331 through 1910.335) require training for employees who face a risk of electric shock that the installation does not reduce to a safe level. The training may be classroom or on-the-job, and the degree of training depends on the risk.
  • NFPA 70E explains how to meet it. OSHA has written that it may use NFPA 70E to support citations under its own standards, and that compliance with NFPA 70E is an acceptable means of hazard abatement.
  • Industry treats 70E as the baseline. Employers, insurers, and data center customers commonly expect an electrical safety program built on it.

Who needs NFPA 70E training?

Anyone who could be exposed to an electrical hazard needs training, but the depth depends on whether the employer designates them as qualified for a given task. OSHA defines a qualified person as one who has received training in, and has demonstrated skills and knowledge in, the construction and operation of electric equipment and installations and the hazards involved. The same person can be qualified for some equipment and unqualified for other equipment.

Unqualified does not mean untrained. An IT technician racking a server next to a live remote power panel needs to know what not to touch and when to stop. The Data Center Electrical Safety course is written for that audience.

NFPA 70E training requirements: what must be covered

NFPA 70E training must cover the safety-related work practices and procedures the employee will use, matched to the hazards of their job. For qualified persons, the standard spells out a minimum.

A qualified person must be trained in and familiar with:

  • The construction and operation of the equipment or work method, and how to identify and avoid its electrical hazards.
  • Skills and techniques to distinguish exposed energized parts from other parts of the equipment.
  • Skills and techniques to determine the nominal voltage of exposed energized parts.
  • The approach distances in the standard and the voltages they will be exposed to.
  • The decision-making process to plan the job, identify hazards, assess risk, and select controls from the hierarchy of controls, including PPE.
  • Selecting the right test instrument and demonstrating how to test for absence of voltage, including the instrument's limitations.
  • Proper use of precautionary techniques, PPE, insulating and shielding materials, and insulated tools.

NFPA 70E also calls for emergency response training for workers exposed to shock hazards, and it expects the employer to confirm through supervision or periodic inspection that people are actually following the practices they were taught.

How often is NFPA 70E training required?

NFPA 70E requires retraining in safety-related work practices, and in applicable changes to the standard, at intervals not exceeding three years.

Three years is the maximum, not the target. Retraining is also needed sooner when:

  • Supervision or an annual inspection shows a worker is not following safety-related work practices.
  • New technology, new types of equipment, or changed procedures require different practices.
  • A worker must use practices they do not normally use, such as a rare switching task.
  • A worker's job duties change.

In data centers, the second trigger comes up often. A new lithium-ion UPS, a busway retrofit, or a revised switching procedure all justify targeted retraining before the work starts.

Can you take NFPA 70E training online?

Yes, for the knowledge portion. OSHA allows classroom or on-the-job training, and self-paced online courses are a practical classroom option for a 24/7 workforce spread across shifts. What online training cannot do is verify hands-on skill on your equipment.

The strongest programs combine all three: online modules for knowledge, a short instructor session for questions, and a supervisor walking each person through the actual equipment before the employer designates them qualified.

What to look for in an NFPA 70E training course

Look for current-edition content, honest claims about what the course does, and examples from equipment like yours.

  • Edition. The course should reflect a recent edition of NFPA 70E and say which one.
  • Audience fit. Awareness content for unqualified staff; deeper content on risk assessment, PPE, and testing for those working on equipment.
  • Relevant examples. UPS bypass, battery strings, and switchgear teach data center staff more than generic industrial scenes.
  • Honest credential language. Be wary of any course that promises to make you "qualified" or calls itself an OSHA or NFPA certification. Neither OSHA nor NFPA 70E certifies workers through a training course.
  • Records you can use. A verifiable certificate of completion with the course content, learner name, and date makes documentation easier.

What NFPA 70E training documentation should employers keep?

NFPA 70E requires employers to document employee training. The record should show the content of the training, each employee's name, and the dates, and it should be kept for as long as the person is employed.

A practical file for each worker in a data center includes:

  1. Certificates of completion and course outlines for classroom or online training.
  2. Records of site-specific instruction and observed demonstrations, signed by the supervisor.
  3. The employer's written qualification decision, listing the equipment and tasks covered.
  4. Annual field audit results and any retraining that followed.
  5. Lockout/tagout training certification, which OSHA 1910.147 requires separately.

Inspectors, insurers, and colocation customers all ask for these records, so keep them together and easy to retrieve. Our Data Center Safety Checklist includes a training records section.

Frequently asked questions

Is NFPA 70E training required by law?

NFPA 70E is a consensus standard, not a federal regulation, and OSHA does not enforce it directly. OSHA's own electrical rules do require training for workers exposed to electrical hazards, and OSHA treats compliance with NFPA 70E as an acceptable way to control those hazards. Some employers and customers also require it by contract.

Is there an NFPA 70E training certification?

Training providers issue certificates of completion, which document that you finished a course. That is not a license and does not make you a qualified person; only your employer makes that designation. NFPA separately offers the Certified Electrical Safety Compliance Professional (CESCP) credential for people who manage electrical safety programs, so check NFPA for current details.

Can NFPA 70E training be done online?

Yes, for the knowledge portion. Online courses work well for concepts, boundaries, PPE selection, and refreshers. Qualified persons also need site-specific instruction and must demonstrate skills, such as testing for absence of voltage, on the equipment they will work on.

How long is NFPA 70E training good for?

NFPA 70E requires retraining at intervals not exceeding three years. Retraining is needed sooner if a worker's duties change, new equipment or procedures are introduced, or supervision shows the worker is not following safe practices.

Do data center IT technicians need NFPA 70E training?

They need electrical safety training suited to their exposure. IT technicians work in racks fed by live power and often near panels and PDUs, so they should understand electrical hazards, approach boundaries, lockout/tagout rules, and when to stop and call a qualified electrician.

Is arc flash training the same as NFPA 70E training?

Arc flash training is one part of NFPA 70E training. A full NFPA 70E program also covers shock hazards, electrically safe work conditions, lockout/tagout, energized work permits, test equipment, and emergency response.

**Short answer:** Business continuity training teaches how to keep critical operations running through a disruption. It covers the business impact analysis (BIA), recovery time and recovery point objectives (RTO and RPO), continuity strategies, writing the business continuity plan, disaster recovery for IT, crisis management, and testing through exercises. Recognized certifications include DRI International's CBCP and the Business Continuity Institute's CBCI, and the main international standard is ISO 22301.

Key takeaways

- Business continuity covers the whole organization; disaster recovery covers restoring IT systems and data.
- The business impact analysis drives everything: it sets which processes matter most and how fast they must recover.
- RTO is how quickly a process must be back; RPO is how much data loss is acceptable.
- DRI International (ABCP, CBCP, MBCP) and the BCI (CBCI) offer the best-known professional certifications.
- A plan nobody has exercised is a document, not a capability. Training should end in a tabletop or live exercise.

## What business continuity and disaster recovery training covers

- **Program management:** policy, scope, governance, and roles for a business continuity management system.
- **Risk assessment:** identifying threats such as power loss, cyberattack, severe weather, supplier failure, and pandemic.
- **Business impact analysis:** identifying critical processes, their dependencies, and the impact of disruption over time.
- **Strategy:** choosing recovery approaches such as alternate sites, redundant data centers, cloud recovery, and manual workarounds.
- **Plan development:** writing business continuity, disaster recovery, and crisis communication plans that people can follow under stress.
- **IT disaster recovery:** backup, replication, failover, and the order in which systems are restored.
- **Exercises and maintenance:** tabletop and live exercises, after-action reviews, and keeping plans current.

## Key business continuity concepts

**Business continuity terms**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| BIA (business impact analysis) | Analysis of what each process does, what it depends on, and how losses grow over time when it stops |
| RTO (recovery time objective) | The target time to restore a process or system after a disruption |
| RPO (recovery point objective) | The maximum acceptable data loss, measured back in time from the disruption |
| MTPD (maximum tolerable period of disruption) | The point after which the impact becomes unacceptable |
| BCP (business continuity plan) | The documented procedures for continuing critical operations during a disruption |
| DRP (disaster recovery plan) | The documented procedures for restoring IT systems and data |

## Business continuity planning certifications

- **DRI International:** Associate Business Continuity Professional (ABCP), Certified Business Continuity Professional (CBCP), and Master Business Continuity Professional (MBCP), with experience requirements increasing at each level.
- **Business Continuity Institute (BCI):** Certificate of the BCI (CBCI), the entry credential for BCI membership grades.
- **ISO 22301 lead implementer and lead auditor:** course-based certifications from accredited training bodies for people who build or audit a business continuity management system.
- **IT disaster recovery credentials** from vendors and IT certification bodies for technical recovery roles.

Course certificates, such as those from our online courses, document training in continuity methods and are a common first step before a professional certification.

## Business continuity standards and frameworks

- **ISO 22301:** the international standard for business continuity management systems, which organizations can be certified against.
- **NFPA 1660:** the NFPA standard for emergency, continuity, and crisis management, which consolidated the former NFPA 1600.
- **FFIEC Business Continuity Management booklet:** expectations for U.S. financial institutions and their service providers.
- **NIST SP 800-34:** contingency planning guide for federal information systems.

## Business continuity for data centers

For a data center operator, continuity has two sides. The facility must keep running through power, cooling, and staffing disruptions, which is the job of redundancy, maintenance, and emergency operating procedures. And the operator must support its customers' continuity plans by meeting SLAs, communicating clearly during incidents, and documenting recovery capabilities for audits. Training for data center teams should cover both, including how the facility's [Tier level and redundancy](https://datacentertraining.us/learn/data-center-tiers.html) relate to customer RTOs.

## Business continuity exercises and workshops

Training sticks when people practice. A practical sequence is a walkthrough of the plan with each team, a tabletop exercise where leaders talk through a realistic scenario, a functional exercise where teams carry out parts of the plan, and a full exercise such as an actual failover. Each ends with an after-action review and plan updates. Online courses prepare participants so exercise time is spent on decisions, not definitions.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is business continuity training?

Business continuity training teaches how to keep critical operations running during a disruption. It covers risk assessment, business impact analysis, recovery objectives, continuity strategies, writing plans, IT disaster recovery, crisis communication, and exercising the plan.

### What is the best business continuity certification?

The most widely recognized are DRI International's CBCP (with ABCP for newer practitioners and MBCP for senior ones) and the Business Continuity Institute's CBCI. ISO 22301 lead implementer and lead auditor certifications suit people building or auditing a formal management system.

### What is the difference between business continuity and disaster recovery?

Business continuity covers keeping the whole organization operating, including people, facilities, suppliers, and processes. Disaster recovery is the part focused on restoring IT systems and data after a disruption.

### Can I take business continuity training online?

Yes. Business continuity concepts, methods, and plan development are well suited to online, self-paced courses. Exercises and workshops then let teams practice with their own plans.

### What is ISO 22301?

ISO 22301 is the international standard that specifies requirements for a business continuity management system. Organizations can be certified against it by an accredited certification body.

Credential Transparency

What Your Certificate Does & Doesn't Mean

Upon completion, learners receive a certificate of completion for the selected course. This documents completion of training content aligned with the applicable industry standard or framework named on the course, such as ANSI/TIA-942, the Uptime Institute Tier framework, NFPA 70E, or SOC 2/ISO 27001. It does not mean any standards body has certified the student, endorsed the course, or issued a license. Employers remain responsible for determining whether employees are trained, qualified, and competent for their assigned duties and site conditions.

Certificate of Completion

Proof a learner finished the course content. Not a government-issued credential or license.

Standards-Aligned Training

Course content is designed around applicable industry standards and frameworks. "Aligned with" is not the same as "issued by" or "approved by" that standards body.

Employer-Required Documentation

Employers must keep their own training records showing who is qualified for which role at their specific site.

IACET Provider Accreditation

HAZWOPER OSHA Training, LLC, the parent company, is an IACET Accredited Provider. These data center courses do not carry IACET CEUs.

IACET & Continuing Education Credit

Do These Courses Earn CEUs?

No. HAZWOPER OSHA Training, LLC, the parent company that delivers Data Center Training, is an IACET Accredited Provider. That accreditation belongs to the company. The data center courses on this site do not carry IACET CEUs, and learners do not earn CEUs for completing them.

The courses are also not approved for PDHs or any other continuing education credit from an engineering board, BICSI, or another professional body. What learners receive is a certificate of completion for each course they finish.

If you need CEU- or PDH-eligible coursework for a license or professional certification, confirm the requirement with your board before enrolling.

Certificate Verification

Verify a Learner's Certificate

Employers, site operators, and general contractors can check any certificate issued for these courses online.

How to verify

  1. Find the unique certificate number and issue date printed on the certificate of completion.
  2. Enter them on HAZWOPER OSHA Training's certificate verification page.
  3. Compare the result with the name and course on the certificate you were given.

What a successful lookup confirms

That the learner named on the certificate enrolled in, completed, and passed the course listed on the certificate, issued by HAZWOPER OSHA Training, LLC.

What it does not confirm

It does not show that the person is qualified or authorized to do specific work at your site, holds a license, or earned CEU or PDH credit. Those decisions stay with the employer.

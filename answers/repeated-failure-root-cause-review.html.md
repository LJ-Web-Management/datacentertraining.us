**Short answer:** Repeated failures warrant a structured review of timeline, operating context, maintenance history, control logic, and contributing conditions instead of treating each reset as an isolated event.

## Why it matters

A pattern of repeated resets can normalize a fault and allow risk to accumulate.

## What to check

- Define recurrence threshold for review
- Preserve comparable event data
- Separate confirmed cause from hypothesis
- Track corrective actions to verified closure

## Example from the field

The same fan alarm returns every weekend. A cross-shift review finds the timing coincides with a scheduled sequence change and assigns a controls investigation.

## Follow-up question

### When should a repeated alarm trigger a root-cause review?

When recurrence, consequence, or uncertainty exceeds the facility's escalation threshold; the threshold should be defined in policy.

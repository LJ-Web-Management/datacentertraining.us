**Short answer:** A camera coverage review should map intended views, blind spots, retention and access ownership, and any compensating control needed for critical doors or routes.

## Why it matters

A camera on a plan does not prove it provides usable coverage in actual lighting, layout, or operating conditions.

## What to check

- Walk the approved camera map
- Test views for obstruction and lighting
- Document blind areas and purpose
- Route repositioning through security change control

## Example from the field

A new rack blocks a corridor camera's intended view. Security records the gap and coordinates a revised view before the project closes.

## Follow-up question

### Does installing a camera eliminate the need for access controls?

No. Cameras support detection and review but do not replace authorization, barriers, or response procedures.

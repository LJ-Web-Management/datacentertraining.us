**Short answer:** A useful generator readiness review combines status, alarms, maintenance exceptions, fuel-support information, and corrective-action ownership rather than relying on a single green indicator.

## Why it matters

A status light can hide unresolved issues if the team does not connect inspection findings to action and accountability.

## What to check

- Review status and alarm history
- Confirm open maintenance exceptions
- Assign owners and due dates
- Escalate any readiness uncertainty

## Example from the field

A weekly meeting finds three open generator actions with no assigned owner. The facility manager assigns responsibility and tracks closure evidence.

## Follow-up question

### What makes a generator readiness review useful?

It connects equipment evidence to a clear readiness decision, named owner, and follow-up date.

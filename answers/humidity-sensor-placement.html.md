**Short answer:** Humidity sensors should be placed to represent the conditions the facility intends to monitor, with known location and airflow context; an average may hide local variation.

## Why it matters

Poor placement can lead teams to adjust systems based on a condition that does not represent equipment exposure.

## What to check

- Map sensors to zones and airflow
- Review readings against nearby points
- Document relocation or replacement
- Use approved criteria for changes

## Example from the field

A hallway sensor reports stable humidity while a perimeter area changes. The facilities engineer checks placement and zone representation before adjusting humidification.

## Follow-up question

### Can one humidity sensor represent a large data hall?

Only if the design and measurement purpose support that assumption; confirm coverage with engineering.

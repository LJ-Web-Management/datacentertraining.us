**Short answer:** Strainer service on a secondary cooling loop should be planned around isolation, stored pressure, fluid handling, affected loads, and qualified personnel using the approved equipment procedure.

## Why it matters

A maintenance task that looks small can introduce leakage, contamination, or cooling interruption.

## What to check

- Identify the correct loop and isolation points
- Confirm maintenance scope and load impact
- Plan fluid containment and cleanliness controls
- Verify return-to-service evidence

## Example from the field

A differential pressure trend suggests a strainer may need service. The planner coordinates the work window with IT load owners before any isolation is scheduled.

## Follow-up question

### Can a technician clean a strainer while the loop is still supporting live loads?

Only if an approved engineered procedure explicitly allows it and authorized personnel confirm the controls.

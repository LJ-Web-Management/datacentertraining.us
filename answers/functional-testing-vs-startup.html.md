**Short answer:** Equipment startup confirms a unit can be placed into service under its procedure; functional testing evaluates whether it responds as intended within a broader operating sequence.

## Why it matters

Confusing these activities can leave integrated dependencies untested even though individual devices run.

## What to check

- Define objective for each test
- Identify controls and interfaces included
- List prerequisites and expected responses
- Track gaps from component test to integrated test

## Example from the field

A chiller starts successfully, but no one has tested its response to a BMS demand change. The commissioning plan separates startup evidence from functional sequence testing.

## Follow-up question

### Does successful startup prove integrated operation?

No. It proves a narrower equipment condition; integrated behavior requires tests designed for system interactions.

**Short answer:** Maintenance frequency should reconcile manufacturer guidance, applicable requirements, duty conditions, failure history, redundancy, and the site's risk-based maintenance policy.

## Why it matters

Blindly extending or shortening intervals can increase risk or create unnecessary interventions.

## What to check

- Record the basis for each interval
- Review condition and failure trends
- Assess impact of taking equipment out of service
- Approve interval changes through governance

## Example from the field

A team proposes delaying inspections because a unit has run without alarms. The planner compares OEM guidance and condition evidence before approving any interval change.

## Follow-up question

### Can a quiet alarm history justify extending maintenance intervals?

Not by itself; use a documented risk review and applicable manufacturer and regulatory information.

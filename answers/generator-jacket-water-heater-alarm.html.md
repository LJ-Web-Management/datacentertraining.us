**Short answer:** A jacket-water heater alarm can indicate a condition that affects engine temperature and readiness, so teams should verify the alarm, unit status, and maintenance response through approved controls.

## Why it matters

A cold engine or failed heater may affect start performance; ignoring the alarm can create a false impression of readiness.

## What to check

- Log alarm and affected unit
- Check redundancy and current readiness status
- Notify the responsible generator technician
- Track repair and retest evidence

## Example from the field

A night-shift alarm appears on one generator while the remaining units show normal status. The operator documents the equipment affected and follows the site notification matrix.

## Follow-up question

### Does a heater alarm mean the generator cannot start?

Not by itself; it signals a condition requiring assessment. Use manufacturer guidance and site procedures to determine readiness rather than assuming either failure or normal operation.

**Short answer:** A condensate alarm should prompt teams to identify the affected unit, nearby leak risk, and cooling impact while using the site's escalation and water-management procedures.

## Why it matters

Overflow or an unrecognized drain issue can damage adjacent equipment and create slip or electrical exposure risks.

## What to check

- Locate the affected unit using approved displays
- Check for visible water from a safe position
- Protect nearby loads under site procedures
- Record alarm and corrective-action owner

## Example from the field

A unit reports a high condensate level near a customer area. The technician notifies the operations lead and documents the isolation decision made by authorized staff.

## Follow-up question

### Should an operator open a cooling unit to clear a drain alarm?

Only personnel authorized and trained for that task should access the unit; first-response observations should stay within assigned limits.

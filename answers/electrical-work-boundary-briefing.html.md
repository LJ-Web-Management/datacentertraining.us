**Short answer:** A boundary briefing tells affected staff where work is occurring, who controls the area, what restrictions apply, and how to report a changing condition.

## Why it matters

People who do not understand the work zone may enter it, interrupt the task, or miss a changing hazard.

## What to check

- Name the work lead and affected equipment
- Mark access restrictions clearly
- Explain alternate routes and contact method
- Repeat the briefing when scope changes

## Example from the field

A cleaning crew is scheduled near a switchgear work zone. The supervisor coordinates access timing and routes before the electrical team begins.

## Follow-up question

### Does a boundary briefing qualify someone to perform electrical work?

No. It communicates restrictions; task qualification and authorization remain separate requirements.

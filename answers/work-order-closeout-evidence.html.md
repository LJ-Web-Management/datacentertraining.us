What a work order closeout should record: work done, asset, procedure, findings, parts, measurements, deviations, and who accepted the return to service.

**Short answer:** A strong closeout records what was done, the asset and procedure used, findings, parts, measurements where required, deviations, and the person who accepted return to service.

## Why it matters

A vague “complete” status gives future planners little evidence about equipment condition or repeated defects.

## What to check

- Identify asset and work scope
- Record findings and deviations
- Attach required measurement or test evidence
- Name the acceptance owner and remaining actions

## Example from the field

A work order notes “pump serviced” but omits the seal observation. The reliability lead reopens it for the evidence needed to plan the next inspection.

## Follow-up question

### What should a maintenance closeout always identify?

At minimum, the asset, scope, result, deviations, and unresolved follow-up, based on the site's record requirements.

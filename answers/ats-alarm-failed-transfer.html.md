**Short answer:** Treat an ATS alarm as a possible failed or incomplete transfer until qualified staff confirm source status, switch position, load condition, and event sequence using approved procedures.

## Why it matters

An unverified transfer can leave critical loads on an unstable source or create conflicting operator actions during a power event.

## What to check

- Capture the alarm timestamp and sequence
- Confirm normal and alternate source indications through approved displays
- Compare switch position with downstream power status
- Escalate mismatches to the designated electrical authority

## Example from the field

During a utility disturbance, the ATS reports a transfer attempt but downstream monitoring does not match the expected source. The team records the event and follows the site escalation plan rather than issuing repeated commands.

## Follow-up question

### What should operators do when ATS indications disagree?

Pause discretionary actions, preserve alarm and event data, notify the authorized electrical lead, and follow the site's approved response procedure.

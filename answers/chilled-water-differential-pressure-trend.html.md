**Short answer:** A differential-pressure trend is useful only when the sensor location, operating state, and control sequence are understood; compare the reading with independent indicators before treating it as a system problem.

## Why it matters

A bad sensor or changed operating mode can drive unnecessary adjustments and distract teams from the actual restriction or control fault.

## What to check

- Confirm the sensor location and current mode
- Compare with a known reference or nearby measurements
- Review recent maintenance and setpoint changes
- Escalate readings outside the approved operating envelope

## Example from the field

A pressure trend falls after a valve project. The technician checks the sensor impulse line and sequence history before the team changes pump settings.

## Follow-up question

### What should a technician check before acting on a pressure reading?

Verify what the sensor measures, whether its input is plausible, and what operating condition existed when it was recorded.

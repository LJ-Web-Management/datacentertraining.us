# What Controls Should Apply When an Alarm Is Shelved?

Field Answers · Monitoring & Smart Facility

The controls alarm shelving needs in a critical facility: authorization, visibility, reason codes, time limits, and an owner who confirms the alarm is restored.

**Short answer:** Alarm shelving should be temporary, authorized, visible, reason-coded, and time-limited, with a defined owner who confirms the condition is restored or the suppression is removed.

## Why it matters

Untracked shelving can hide a developing issue across shifts and create false confidence in monitoring coverage.

## What to check

- Require reason and approver
- Set an expiry or review time
- Display shelved status to operators
- Verify unshelving and closure evidence

## Example from the field

A nuisance alarm is shelved during a scheduled test. The shift log records the test window and the person responsible for restoring normal monitoring.

## Follow-up question

### Should an alarm remain shelved after a test ends?

No. The owner should verify the condition and restore normal alarm handling under the procedure.

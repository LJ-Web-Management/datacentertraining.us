**Short answer:** A UPS mode change should be treated as a controlled operational change with an authorized owner, understood impact, communication plan, and verified return condition.

## Why it matters

Unreviewed mode changes can alter protection, redundancy, or maintenance assumptions even when the display shows no immediate alarm.

## What to check

- Identify the reason and approver
- Assess affected loads and redundancy
- Communicate before the change
- Record the expected and verified final mode

## Example from the field

A technician proposes a mode change to investigate an alarm. The shift lead checks the approved procedure, maintenance window, and rollback criteria before authorizing action.

## Follow-up question

### Should an operator change UPS mode to clear an alarm?

No. Diagnose and escalate under approved procedures; an alarm reset or mode change must not substitute for understanding the cause.
